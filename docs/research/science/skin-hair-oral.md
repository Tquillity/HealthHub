# Skin, hair and oral health: evidence-based science

Sun protection, eczema, acne, common skin infections, hair loss and dental care from NHS, NICE, Cochrane and dermatology bodies.

## Sun and skin cancer

### Sunscreen and sun-safe behaviour
- **Use:** Preventing sunburn, skin ageing and skin cancer
- **What it is / how it's used:** Shade 11am-3pm, clothing/hat/sunglasses, broad-spectrum SPF 30+ with high UVA protection, applied generously (~2 tablespoons for the body) 15-30 min before going out and reapplied every 2 hours and after swimming. Babies under 6 months out of direct sun.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; patch-test if sensitive skin.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Sunscreen and sun safety](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/seasonal-health/sunscreen-and-sun-safety/), [AAD: How to select a sunscreen](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/sun-protection/shade-clothing-sunscreen/how-to-select-sunscreen)
- **HealthHub fit:** Dashboard: UV index + reapply reminder routine on sunny days.

### Mole checks (ABCDE)
- **Use:** Early melanoma detection
- **What it is / how it's used:** Look for Asymmetry, irregular Border, multiple Colours, Diameter >6 mm, Evolving (changing) moles; also new moles in adults, itching or bleeding. USPSTF finds insufficient evidence for routine clinician skin screening in adults without symptoms, but changing moles should be checked.
- **Evidence:** Proven (prompt assessment of changing lesions)
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [AAD: ABCDEs of melanoma](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/find/at-risk/abcdes), [NHS: Melanoma](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/melanoma-skin-cancer/), [USPSTF: Skin cancer screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/skin-cancer-screening)

## Eczema and inflammatory skin

### Emollients as the base of eczema care
- **Use:** Atopic eczema
- **What it is / how it's used:** Use leave-on emollients liberally and often, including when skin is clear; use as soap substitute; topical corticosteroids for flares, stepped by severity (NICE CG57). Identify triggers (soaps, wool, heat).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Paraffin-based emollients are a fire risk on fabrics; steroids safe when used as directed (fear of steroids leads to undertreatment).
- **Sources:** [NICE CG57 Eczema in under 12s](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg57), [NHS: Atopic eczema](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/atopic-eczema/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine: twice-daily emollient reminder; flare diary with photo and trigger tags.

### Psoriasis basics
- **Use:** Plaque psoriasis
- **What it is / how it's used:** Emollients plus topical vitamin D analogues and corticosteroids (Cochrane: both effective, combination best); triggers include stress, smoking, alcohol. Linked with higher cardiovascular risk.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Avoid strong steroids on face/folds long term.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: topical treatments for psoriasis](https://www.cochrane.org/CD005028), [NHS: Psoriasis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/psoriasis/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Flare diary.

### Contact dermatitis
- **Use:** Rash from irritants/allergens
- **What it is / how it's used:** Identify and avoid the trigger (detergents, nickel, fragrances, hair dye), wear gloves for wet work, emollients, short steroid course.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Contact dermatitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/contact-dermatitis/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: product exposure tags against rash entries.

## Acne and rosacea

### Acne treatment ladder
- **Use:** Acne
- **What it is / how it's used:** Mild: pharmacy benzoyl peroxide or adapalene, 6-8 weeks to see effect; don't squeeze; non-comedogenic products. Moderate-severe or scarring: GP (topical combinations, oral antibiotics + topical, hormonal options, isotretinoin via specialist). Diet links (high-GI foods, dairy) are weak.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Benzoyl peroxide bleaches fabric; retinoids not in pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Acne](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acne/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Teen-friendly routine with an 8-week "give it time" progress tracker.

### Rosacea triggers
- **Use:** Facial flushing/redness
- **What it is / how it's used:** Daily sunscreen, avoid personal triggers (alcohol, spicy food, hot drinks, heat), gentle skincare; prescription creams help.
- **Evidence:** Proven (treatments); trigger avoidance Promising
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Rosacea](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rosacea/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Trigger journal.

## Hair and scalp

### Hair loss: what is normal and what helps
- **Use:** Thinning hair, patchy loss
- **What it is / how it's used:** Losing 50-100 hairs/day is normal; temporary shedding after stress/illness/birth recovers. Minoxidil is the evidence-based OTC option for pattern hair loss. See GP for sudden or patchy loss, itchy/burning scalp. Check iron/thyroid if diffuse loss.
- **Evidence:** Proven (minoxidil); most "hair growth" supplements unproven
- **Safety:** Minoxidil not in pregnancy; biotin supplements can distort lab tests (e.g. thyroid, troponin).
- **Sources:** [NHS: Hair loss](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hair-loss/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results: ferritin/thyroid entries; caution note on biotin before blood tests.

### Dandruff
- **Use:** Flaky scalp
- **What it is / how it's used:** Anti-dandruff shampoos (ketoconazole, zinc pyrithione, selenium, salicylic acid, coal tar) used regularly, try each for a month.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Dandruff](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dandruff/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library.

## Common skin infections

### Athlete's foot, fungal nails, cold sores, warts
- **Use:** Common minor skin infections
- **What it is / how it's used:** Athlete's foot: pharmacy antifungal, dry between toes, don't share towels. Fungal nails: slow; antifungal paint/oral via GP. Cold sores: antiviral cream at tingling stage, avoid kissing babies. Warts: often resolve alone; salicylic acid or freezing.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** People with diabetes: foot problems need GP review.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Athlete's foot](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/athletes-foot/), [NHS: Fungal nail infection](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fungal-nail-infection/), [NHS: Cold sores](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cold-sores/), [NHS: Warts and verrucas](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/warts-and-verrucas/)

## Teeth and gums

### Brushing with fluoride toothpaste
- **Use:** Preventing tooth decay
- **What it is / how it's used:** Brush twice daily for 2 minutes (last thing at night + one other time) with 1,350-1,500 ppm fluoride toothpaste for adults; spit, don't rinse. Cochrane: fluoride toothpaste prevents caries, higher concentration more effective.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Children: smear (under 3) or pea-sized (3-6) amount, supervised.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: fluoride toothpaste strengths](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007868), [NHS: How to keep your teeth clean](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: morning/evening brushing with 2-min timer for kids.

### Electric vs manual toothbrush
- **Use:** Plaque and gum health
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: powered (especially rotation-oscillation) brushes reduce plaque and gingivitis more than manual brushes in the short and long term.
- **Evidence:** Proven (modest benefit)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: powered vs manual toothbrushes](https://www.cochrane.org/CD002281)
- **HealthHub fit:** Groceries: brush-head replacement reminder every 3 months.

### Gum disease and interdental cleaning
- **Use:** Bleeding gums, bad breath
- **What it is / how it's used:** Bleeding gums when brushing are an early sign of gum disease; clean between teeth daily (interdental brushes/floss), stop smoking, regular dental check-ups. Gum disease is linked to diabetes.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Gum disease](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gum-disease/), [NHS: Bad breath](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bad-breath/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Dental check-up reminders per household member.

### Sugar frequency and tooth decay
- **Use:** Preventing cavities
- **What it is / how it's used:** How often sugar is eaten matters most: keep sugary food/drinks to mealtimes, water/milk between meals.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Tooth decay](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/tooth-decay/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner: flag between-meal sugary snacks for kids.

## Red flags

### Skin and mouth red flags
- **Use:** When to see a doctor/dentist
- **What it is / how it's used:** Changing or bleeding mole; mouth ulcer or red/white patch not healed in 3 weeks; lump in mouth/neck; spreading red hot painful skin with fever (cellulitis); rash with fever that doesn't fade under a glass (sepsis/meningitis: emergency); facial swelling from a tooth infection affecting eye or breathing.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Mouth cancer symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/mouth-cancer/symptoms/), [NHS: Mouth ulcers](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/mouth-ulcers/), [NHS: Melanoma](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/melanoma-skin-cancer/)
