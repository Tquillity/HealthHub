# Mental health and stress: evidence-based science

Self-help and lifestyle measures backed by NICE, Cochrane, WHO and NHS for stress, anxiety and low mood, plus supplements that do and don't work and crisis red flags.

## Everyday wellbeing

### Five steps to mental wellbeing
- **Use:** Prevention, general wellbeing
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS framework: connect with others, be physically active, learn new skills, give to others, pay attention to the present moment (mindfulness).
- **Evidence:** Promising (evidence-informed framework, from Foresight review; individual elements have trial support)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: 5 steps to mental wellbeing](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/five-steps-to-mental-wellbeing/), [NIMH: Caring for your mental health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: weekly "5 ways" check-in with one micro-goal per step.

### Mood and anxiety self-check (PHQ-2/PHQ-9, GAD-2/GAD-7)
- **Use:** Noticing depression/anxiety early; tracking change
- **What it is / how it's used:** Validated short questionnaires used in primary care. USPSTF recommends depression screening for all adults and anxiety screening for adults under 65. Scores of 10+ on PHQ-9 or GAD-7 suggest talking to a GP.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Not a diagnosis. Item 9 of PHQ-9 (thoughts of self-harm) must trigger crisis information.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Depression and suicide risk screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-depression-suicide-risk-adults), [USPSTF: Anxiety screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/anxiety-adults-screening)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: monthly optional PHQ-9/GAD-7 with trend chart and safety routing.

## Low mood and depression

### Exercise for depression
- **Use:** Mild-moderate depression, low mood
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: exercise moderately reduces depressive symptoms vs control. NICE NG222 lists group exercise as a first-line option for less severe depression. BMJ 2024 network meta-analysis found walking/jogging, yoga and strength training among the most effective, with greater effects at higher intensity.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: exercise for depression](https://www.cochrane.org/CD004366), [NICE NG222 Depression in adults](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)
- **HealthHub fit:** Link fitness logs to mood ratings; "move for mood" routine.

### Guided self-help CBT and behavioural activation
- **Use:** Less severe depression and anxiety
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE first-line options include guided self-help (book or digital CBT with practitioner support), group CBT and behavioural activation (scheduling meaningful/enjoyable activities). Available free via NHS Talking Therapies (self-referral).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; severe depression needs clinician care.
- **Sources:** [NICE NG222](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222), [NICE CG113 Anxiety and panic](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg113), [NHS Talking Therapies](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/talking-therapies-medicine-treatments/talking-therapies-and-counselling/nhs-talking-therapies/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: behavioural activation planner (plan 1 pleasant + 1 achievement activity daily, rate mood after).

### St John's wort
- **Use:** Mild-moderate depression
- **What it is / how it's used:** Some evidence of benefit similar to antidepressants for mild-moderate depression, but product strength varies.
- **Evidence:** Promising
- **Safety:** Dangerous interactions: reduces effect of the contraceptive pill, anticoagulants, HIV drugs, transplant drugs; serotonin syndrome with SSRIs. NICE advises clinicians not to recommend it for this reason.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: St John's wort](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort)
- **HealthHub fit:** Medication log interaction warning (esp. contraceptive pill).

### Tryptophan / 5-HTP for depression
- **Use:** Low mood
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: too few quality studies; insufficient evidence of effect.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (unproven; risk with antidepressants)
- **Safety:** Serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: tryptophan and 5-HTP for depression](https://www.cochrane.org/CD003198)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check card.

## Stress and anxiety

### Slow breathing exercise
- **Use:** Acute stress, anxiety, panic
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS method: breathe in through the nose for ~5 counts, out through the mouth for ~5 counts, for 3-5 minutes, letting the belly expand.
- **Evidence:** Promising (small trials; widely recommended)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Breathing exercises for stress](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Animated breathing pacer reusable in the timer feature.

### Mindfulness meditation
- **Use:** Stress, anxiety, preventing depression relapse
- **What it is / how it's used:** Mindfulness-based programmes (8-week MBSR/MBCT) reduce anxiety, depression and stress; MBCT is NICE-recommended to prevent relapse in recurrent depression.
- **Evidence:** Proven (MBCT for relapse prevention); Promising for general stress
- **Safety:** Generally low risk; occasionally worsens trauma symptoms.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Meditation and mindfulness](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-what-you-need-to-know), [NICE NG222](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)
- **HealthHub fit:** Short guided sessions in routines; tie to the timer.

### Anxiety self-help (GAD)
- **Use:** Persistent worry
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS self-help: self-help CBT courses, regular exercise, cut caffeine/alcohol, scheduled "worry time", breathing exercises, peer support. Stepped care per NICE CG113.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: GAD self-help](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/generalised-anxiety-disorder/self-help/), [NICE CG113](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg113)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: "worry time" scheduled prompt that collects worries and asks which are actionable.

### Panic attack coping
- **Use:** Panic attacks
- **What it is / how it's used:** Stay where you are, slow breathing, remind yourself it will pass (usually 5-20 min) and is not dangerous; CBT is the main treatment for panic disorder.
- **Evidence:** Proven (CBT)
- **Safety:** First episode of chest pain should be checked medically.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Panic disorder](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/)
- **HealthHub fit:** One-tap "panic" screen with breathing pacer and grounding steps.

### Ashwagandha
- **Use:** Stress, sleep
- **What it is / how it's used:** Some small trials show reduced stress/anxiety scores over ~8 weeks; long-term safety unknown.
- **Evidence:** Promising
- **Safety:** Rare liver injury reported; avoid in pregnancy, thyroid disease, and with sedatives/immunosuppressants. Denmark has banned it.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Ashwagandha](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library with safety banner.

### Stress management basics
- **Use:** Work/life stress, burnout prevention
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS: identify stress triggers, plan and prioritise, talk to others, physical activity, limit alcohol/caffeine, protect sleep, notice early signs (irritability, poor sleep, headaches).
- **Evidence:** Promising (guidance-based)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Get help with stress](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/stress/), [NHS Every Mind Matters](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Daily stress rating (0-10) in the journal; dashboard correlations with sleep and activity.

## Red flags (urgent help)

### Crisis and urgent signs
- **Use:** Suicidal thoughts, self-harm, crisis
- **What it is / how it's used:** Get urgent help if someone has thoughts of ending their life, has self-harmed, hears/sees things others don't, or cannot keep themselves safe. Call emergency services (999/112) or crisis lines (e.g. Samaritans 116 123 UK; Mind 90101 / Självmordslinjen 90101 Sweden). Low mood lasting more than 2 weeks warrants a GP visit.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline pathways)
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Help for suicidal thoughts](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/behaviours/help-for-suicidal-thoughts/), [NHS: Depression in adults](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/depression-in-adults/overview/), [WHO: Depression](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/depression)
- **HealthHub fit:** Persistent "Need help now?" link with locale-specific numbers; triggered automatically by journal keywords or PHQ-9 item 9.
