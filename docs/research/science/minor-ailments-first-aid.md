# Minor ailments and first aid: evidence-based science

Home first aid and minor-ailment care from NHS and St John Ambulance guidance, including popular folk fixes that are harmful.

## Burns and wounds

### Burns: cool running water for 20 minutes
- **Use:** Burns and scalds
- **What it is / how it's used:** Remove from heat; cool under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes; remove jewellery/clothing unless stuck; cover loosely with cling film or a clean plastic bag; paracetamol/ibuprofen.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Never ice, butter, toothpaste or creams (Disproven / likely harmful). A&E for chemical/electrical burns, large or deep burns, burns on face/hands/feet/genitals, any burn in babies/young children that is bigger than a coin, or blistering burns.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Burns and scalds](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/)
- **HealthHub fit:** First-aid card with a 20-minute cooling timer (PomoZen engine).

### Cuts and grazes
- **Use:** Minor wounds
- **What it is / how it's used:** Stop bleeding with firm pressure using a clean cloth; rinse under running tap water; pat dry, apply sterile dressing; keep clean.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Get help if bleeding won't stop after 10 min pressure, wound gaping/deep, object embedded, animal/human bite, signs of infection, or unsure of tetanus status.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)
- **HealthHub fit:** First-aid card; household tetanus vaccine record.

### Blisters
- **Use:** Friction blisters
- **What it is / how it's used:** Leave intact and cover with a plaster/blister dressing; if it bursts, don't peel the skin, cover it.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Signs of infection or diabetes: see GP/pharmacist.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Blisters](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/blisters/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library.

### Nosebleeds
- **Use:** Nosebleed
- **What it is / how it's used:** Sit up, lean forward (not back), pinch soft part of the nose for 10-15 min, breathe through mouth; ice pack on bridge.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** A&E if bleeding >10-15 min, after a head injury, very heavy, or on anticoagulants with difficulty stopping.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Nosebleed](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/nosebleed/)
- **HealthHub fit:** First-aid card with 10-min timer.

## Bites, stings and allergy

### Insect bites and stings
- **Use:** Bites/stings
- **What it is / how it's used:** Remove sting by scraping sideways, wash, cold compress, raise area, avoid scratching; antihistamine or mild steroid cream from pharmacy for itch/swelling.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Swelling spreading, signs of infection, or bite in mouth/throat: seek help.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Insect bites and stings](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insect-bites-and-stings/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library.

### Anaphylaxis: adrenaline first
- **Use:** Severe allergic reaction
- **What it is / how it's used:** Signs: swelling of throat/tongue, breathing difficulty, wheeze, dizziness/collapse, pale clammy skin. Use adrenaline auto-injector immediately (outer thigh), call 999/112, lie down with legs raised (sit up if breathing difficult), second dose after 5 min if no improvement.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Antihistamines are not a substitute for adrenaline.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Anaphylaxis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household profiles store allergies and auto-injector expiry dates with reminders.

### Tick bites and Lyme disease
- **Use:** Tick exposure (relevant in Sweden/Europe)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Remove tick promptly with fine-tipped tweezers or a tick tool, grasping close to skin, pull steadily; clean area. Watch 30 days for a spreading bullseye rash or flu-like symptoms.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Rash or symptoms: see GP (antibiotics). TBE vaccine is recommended in risk areas of Sweden.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Lyme disease](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/lyme-disease/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: "tick bite logged" -> 30-day watch reminder with photo.

## Nausea, travel and stomach

### Motion sickness
- **Use:** Travel sickness
- **What it is / how it's used:** Sit where motion is least (front of car, middle of boat), look at the horizon, fresh air, avoid reading; hyoscine or antihistamine tablets taken before travel.
- **Evidence:** Proven (medicines); positioning tips are low risk
- **Safety:** Hyoscine/antihistamines cause drowsiness; age limits for children.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Motion sickness](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/motion-sickness/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Travel checklist.

### Wrist acupressure (P6) for nausea
- **Use:** Nausea and vomiting (post-operative; often tried for travel)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: P6 stimulation reduces post-operative nausea and vomiting vs sham; evidence for motion sickness is weaker.
- **Evidence:** Promising
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: P6 acupoint stimulation](https://www.cochrane.org/CD003281), [NHS: Feeling sick (nausea)](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/feeling-sick-nausea/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library with diagram.

### Food poisoning and dehydration
- **Use:** Vomiting/diarrhoea in adults
- **What it is / how it's used:** Rest, small frequent sips, oral rehydration sachets if at risk; eat when able. Usually settles in a few days.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Blood in stool, signs of severe dehydration, symptoms >7 days, recent travel abroad, pregnancy or weak immunity: seek help.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Food poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/food-poisoning/), [NHS: Dehydration](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipe: home oral rehydration is not advised - suggest pharmacy ORS; fluid log.

### Hiccups
- **Use:** Hiccups
- **What it is / how it's used:** Usually stop on their own within minutes; tricks (sipping cold water, holding breath, breathing into a paper bag briefly) are low-risk but unproven.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal
- **Safety:** Hiccups lasting more than 48 hours: see GP.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Hiccups](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hiccups/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library with "48 h" red flag.

### Piles (haemorrhoids)
- **Use:** Itching, bleeding, discomfort
- **What it is / how it's used:** More fibre and fluids to avoid straining, don't delay going, pharmacy creams for short-term relief.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Rectal bleeding should be checked if new, dark, or with bowel habit change.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Piles](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/piles-haemorrhoids/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Links to fibre tracking.

## Heat, cold and sun

### Heat exhaustion vs heatstroke
- **Use:** Overheating
- **What it is / how it's used:** Heat exhaustion (headache, dizziness, sweating, cramps): move to cool place, lie down with feet raised, drink water/rehydration drinks, cool skin; should improve within 30 min. Heatstroke (no improvement, confusion, fit, not sweating, high temperature): emergency.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Babies, elderly and chronically ill at highest risk.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Heat exhaustion and heatstroke](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Heatwave alert routine for elderly/children in the household.

### Sunburn care
- **Use:** Sunburn
- **What it is / how it's used:** Get out of the sun, cool shower, after-sun or aloe, fluids, painkillers; don't pop blisters.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Blistering over large area, fever, dizziness, or baby sunburnt: seek help.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Sunburn](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sunburn/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library; UV alerts.

### Frostbite
- **Use:** Cold injury (relevant for Nordic winters)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Get warm, rewarm affected area gently in warm (not hot) water for ~30 min; do not rub; avoid refreezing.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Skin hard, pale or blistered: A&E.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Frostbite](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/frostbite/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Winter safety card.

## Emergencies

### Head injury and concussion
- **Use:** Bumps to the head
- **What it is / how it's used:** Ice pack, rest, paracetamol (avoid ibuprofen initially per some advice), have an adult stay for 24 h. A&E for: loss of consciousness, vomiting, seizure, worsening headache, confusion, clear fluid from nose/ears, on blood thinners, or a child who won't stop crying/is unusually drowsy.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Head injury and concussion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/)
- **HealthHub fit:** 24-hour observation checklist after a logged head injury.

### Choking in children and adults
- **Use:** Choking
- **What it is / how it's used:** If coughing, encourage coughing. If not effective: up to 5 back blows, then up to 5 abdominal thrusts (chest thrusts for babies under 1); call 999/112 if not cleared.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** No blind finger sweeps.
- **Sources:** [NHS: How to stop a child from choking](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/how-to-stop-a-child-from-choking/), [NHS/St John Ambulance: First aid advice](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/first-aid/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Offline first-aid cards (choking, CPR, recovery position) accessible without login.

### Poisoning
- **Use:** Swallowed medicines/chemicals
- **What it is / how it's used:** Don't make them vomit; keep the container; call emergency services or poison information (Sweden: Giftinformationscentralen, UK: 111). Store medicines/chemicals out of reach.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Unconscious or breathing difficulties: 999/112.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household medicine cabinet inventory with expiry reminders and poison-centre number.
