# Fitness and movement: evidence-based science

Exercise dosing from WHO, US and NHS guidelines, the dose-response evidence on steps, strength and fitness, and safety signals.

## How much activity

### Adult activity guideline (150-300 min + strength 2x/week)
- **Use:** Baseline target for adults 18-64
- **What it is / how it's used:** WHO 2020: 150-300 min/week moderate or 75-150 min vigorous aerobic activity (or a mix), plus muscle-strengthening for all major muscle groups on 2+ days/week; reduce sitting. All activity counts, even short bouts.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; build up gradually if inactive.
- **Sources:** [WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128), [WHO: Physical activity fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity), [NHS: Activity guidelines 19-64](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/exercise-guidelines/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Dashboard: weekly "active minutes" ring and "strength days" counter per household member.

### Older adult guideline (add balance)
- **Use:** Adults 65+
- **What it is / how it's used:** Same aerobic and strength targets, plus balance and functional training on 3+ days/week to prevent falls (e.g. tai chi, standing on one leg, sit-to-stand).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Start low, go slow; use support for balance work.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Activity guidelines for older adults](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/exercise-guidelines/physical-activity-guidelines-older-adults/), [WHO guidelines on physical activity](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: 10-minute balance routine for older household members.

### Children and teens: 60 min/day
- **Use:** Ages 5-17
- **What it is / how it's used:** Average 60 min/day moderate-vigorous activity across the week, with vigorous and muscle/bone strengthening on 3 days; limit recreational screen time. Under-5s: WHO gives age-specific active play targets (3 h/day for 1-4 years).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Activity guidelines for children and young people](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/exercise-guidelines/physical-activity-guidelines-children-and-young-people/), [WHO: children need to sit less and play more](https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2019-to-grow-up-healthy-children-need-to-sit-less-and-play-more)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household: kid-friendly activity tracker with family challenges.

## Dose-response evidence

### Daily steps
- **Use:** Simple, measurable activity goal
- **What it is / how it's used:** Meta-analysis of 15 cohorts (47,000 adults): mortality falls progressively with more steps, levelling off around 6,000-8,000/day in adults 60+ and 8,000-10,000/day in younger adults. "10,000" is not magic; any increase from a low baseline helps most.
- **Evidence:** Proven (consistent cohort evidence)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Paluch et al. Lancet Public Health 2022 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35247352/), [NHS: Walking for health](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Age-adjusted step goal rather than fixed 10,000; celebrate +1,000/day improvements.

### Muscle-strengthening activity
- **Use:** Lower mortality, diabetes, CVD, cancer risk; healthy ageing
- **What it is / how it's used:** BJSM 2022 meta-analysis: 30-60 min/week of strength activity associated with 10-20% lower all-cause mortality, CVD and cancer; strongest benefit combined with aerobic activity. Bodyweight exercises count.
- **Evidence:** Proven (observational dose-response consistent with trial data on strength)
- **Safety:** Learn technique; start light.
- **Sources:** [Momma et al. BJSM 2022 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35228201/), [NHS: Strength exercises](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: 20-min home strength sessions twice weekly.

### Cardiorespiratory fitness (VO2max) as a vital sign
- **Use:** Longevity, heart health
- **What it is / how it's used:** In 122,000 people undergoing treadmill tests, higher fitness was strongly and continuously linked with lower long-term mortality, with no upper limit of benefit; low fitness carried risk comparable to smoking or diabetes.
- **Evidence:** Proven (large cohort; consistent)
- **Safety:** Low risk; vigorous training if heart disease only with medical clearance.
- **Sources:** [Mandsager et al. JAMA Netw Open 2018 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30646252/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals: store VO2max estimates (watch, step test) and trend them yearly.

### Reduce and break up sitting
- **Use:** Sedentary behaviour
- **What it is / how it's used:** WHO/NHS: limit sedentary time and replace with any intensity activity; high volumes of activity (60-75 min/day moderate) offset risk of long sitting.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Why sitting too much is bad for us](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/why-sitting-too-much-is-bad-for-us/), [WHO: Physical activity fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)
- **HealthHub fit:** Timer: "move break" every 30-60 min of desk time.

## Getting started

### Couch to 5K (graded running programme)
- **Use:** Beginners starting running
- **What it is / how it's used:** 9-week NHS plan with 3 sessions/week alternating walking and running, progressing to 30 minutes of continuous running.
- **Evidence:** Promising (graded progression is evidence-based; programme widely evaluated in observational studies)
- **Safety:** Rest days reduce overuse injury.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/running-and-aerobic-exercises/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Built-in interval routine using the PomoZen timer engine.

### Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans (2nd ed.)
- **Use:** Detailed dosing reference (incl. pregnancy, disability, chronic conditions)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Same 150-300 min aerobic + 2 days strength; highlights immediate benefits (sleep, anxiety, BP) after a single session and that "some is better than none".
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [ODPHP: Physical Activity Guidelines](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines), [Piercy et al. JAMA 2018 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30418471/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Content source for exercise education cards.

### Exercise benefits summary
- **Use:** Motivation
- **What it is / how it's used:** Regular activity reduces risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, some cancers, depression and dementia by roughly 20-35%+.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Benefits of exercise](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/exercise-health-benefits/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Dashboard micro-copy linking each week's activity to benefits.

## Falls and ageing

### Exercise to prevent falls
- **Use:** Older adults at risk of falling
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF recommends exercise interventions for community-dwelling adults 65+ at increased fall risk; NICE NG249 recommends strength and balance programmes and multifactorial assessment after a fall.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Supervised start for frail individuals.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Falls prevention](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/falls-prevention-community-dwelling-older-adults-interventions), [NICE NG249 Falls](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng249), [NHS: Falls prevention](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/falls/prevention/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household elder care: falls risk checklist + balance routine.

## Red flags

### Stop exercising and seek help
- **Use:** Exercise safety
- **What it is / how it's used:** Stop and seek urgent help for chest pain/pressure, unusual breathlessness, fainting, palpitations or irregular heartbeat during exercise. People with heart disease, diabetes complications, or new symptoms should check with a clinician before vigorous training.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Heart attack symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/symptoms/), [ODPHP: Physical Activity Guidelines](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines)
- **HealthHub fit:** Workout logging includes a "felt unwell" option that shows safety guidance.
