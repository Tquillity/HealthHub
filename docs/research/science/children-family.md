# Children and family health: evidence-based science

Babies, children and older relatives: guideline-backed home care, safety, vaccines and the warning signs parents and carers should act on.

## Babies

### Safer sleep to reduce SIDS
- **Use:** Preventing sudden infant death
- **What it is / how it's used:** Always place baby on their back, in a clear flat separate cot in the parents' room for the first 6 months, feet to foot of cot, no pillows/bumpers/loose bedding, no smoking around baby, avoid overheating; never sleep with baby on a sofa or armchair; breastfeeding lowers risk.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Highest-risk co-sleeping: after alcohol, drugs, sedating medicine, smoking, or premature/low-birthweight baby.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Reduce the risk of SIDS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/reduce-the-risk-of-sudden-infant-death-syndrome/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household: new-baby checklist; nursery temperature note (16-20°C).

### Breastfeeding and infant feeding
- **Use:** Infant nutrition
- **What it is / how it's used:** WHO: exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then nutritious complementary foods with continued breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond; no added salt/sugar in baby food.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Formula is safe when breastfeeding isn't possible; prepare per instructions.
- **Sources:** [WHO: Infant and young child feeding](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/infant-and-young-child-feeding)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner baby stage; feeding log.

### Vitamin D (and A, C) for children
- **Use:** Preventing rickets/deficiency
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS: breastfed babies 8.5-10 micrograms vitamin D daily from birth; children 6 months-5 years vitamins A, C and D drops (unless having 500 mL+ formula/day). Everyone 10 micrograms D in autumn/winter.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Don't double up multiple vitamin products.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Vitamins for children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/vitamins-for-children/), [NHS: Vitamin D](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: daily drop reminder per child with age-based dose.

### Colic and teething
- **Use:** Crying baby
- **What it is / how it's used:** Colic: crying 3+ h/day, 3+ days/week in a well baby; usually stops by 3-4 months. Hold/cuddle, gentle motion, white noise; little evidence for drops. Teething: chilled teething rings, sugar-free gels for 4+ months (no teething gels with lidocaine for under 12s; no amber necklaces).
- **Evidence:** Proven (reassurance/soothing); products mostly unproven; amber necklaces Disproven / likely harmful (choking/strangling)
- **Safety:** High-pitched cry, fever, poor feeding, vomiting: see a doctor.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Colic](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/colic/), [NHS: Baby teething symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/teething/baby-teething-symptoms/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cry/sleep log with "when to call" guidance.

## Fever and common illnesses

### Fever at home
- **Use:** Feverish child
- **What it is / how it's used:** Fever (38°C+) is a normal response. Use paracetamol or ibuprofen only if the child is distressed (not just to lower the number); don't alternate routinely; offer fluids; check on them at night; don't sponge with cold water or under-dress excessively. NICE NG143 traffic-light system guides clinicians.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Under 3 months with 38°C+, or 3-6 months with 39°C+: get urgent medical help. Ibuprofen avoided in dehydration/chickenpox per some advice.
- **Sources:** [NHS: High temperature in children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/), [NICE NG143 Fever in under 5s](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng143)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household fever log with age-based thresholds and weight-based dose calculator (with pharmacist-check disclaimer).

### Diarrhoea and vomiting: fluids first
- **Use:** Gastroenteritis
- **What it is / how it's used:** Keep drinking (small frequent sips; continue breast/bottle feeds); oral rehydration solution if at risk of dehydration; stay off school/nursery 48 h after last episode; handwashing with soap (alcohol gel doesn't kill norovirus).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Signs of dehydration (few wet nappies, sunken eyes, drowsiness), blood in stool, green vomit, or vomiting >2 days: get help.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Diarrhoea and vomiting](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/diarrhoea-and-vomiting/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household illness mode: 48-hour exclusion countdown, wet-nappy counter.

### Ear infections: antibiotics rarely needed
- **Use:** Earache in children
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: antibiotics do not reduce pain at 24 h and only slightly at days 2-7; most settle in 3 days. Paracetamol/ibuprofen for pain. Antibiotics more useful under 2 with both ears affected or with discharge.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Swelling behind the ear, high fever, very unwell: urgent care.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: antibiotics for acute otitis media](https://www.cochrane.org/CD000219), [NHS: Ear infections](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ear-infections/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library with "expected course" timeline.

### Croup, bronchiolitis and chickenpox
- **Use:** Common childhood illnesses
- **What it is / how it's used:** Croup: barking cough, keep calm, upright; steroids from GP if needed (steam not recommended). Bronchiolitis: under 2s, usually mild, small frequent feeds. Chickenpox: paracetamol (not ibuprofen), cooling gels, keep off nursery until spots crust.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Breathing difficulty, pauses in breathing, grunting, blue lips, not feeding: emergency.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Croup](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/croup/), [NHS: Bronchiolitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bronchiolitis/), [NHS: Chickenpox](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/chickenpox/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library + exclusion-period guidance.

### Head lice and threadworms
- **Use:** Common household infestations
- **What it is / how it's used:** Lice: wet combing with a detection comb every few days for 2 weeks, or pharmacy treatments; only treat if live lice found. Threadworms: treat the whole household (mebendazole) plus 2 weeks of strict hygiene.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Mebendazole not in pregnancy or under 2 without advice.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Head lice and nits](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-lice-and-nits/), [NHS: Threadworms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/threadworms/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household routine: combing schedule; whole-family treatment checklist.

## Prevention and development

### Childhood vaccination schedule
- **Use:** Preventing serious infections
- **What it is / how it's used:** Follow the national schedule (NHS or Swedish Folkhälsomyndigheten/1177 programme); catch-up is possible if doses are missed.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Mild fever/soreness common; rare allergic reactions managed on site.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Vaccinations and when to have them](https://www.nhs.uk/vaccinations/nhs-vaccinations-and-when-to-have-them/), [1177](https://www.1177.se/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household vaccine record with due-date reminders.

### Fluoride varnish and children's teeth
- **Use:** Preventing tooth decay in children
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: fluoride varnish applied 2-4 times a year reduces decay by roughly 37-43%; supervised brushing with age-appropriate fluoride toothpaste twice daily; first dental visit when teeth appear.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Fluoride varnish](https://www.cochrane.org/CD002279), [NHS: Children's teeth](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/taking-care-of-childrens-teeth/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Child dental routine + dentist reminders.

### Screen time and active play
- **Use:** Healthy development
- **What it is / how it's used:** WHO: under 1s no screen time; 2-4 years no more than 1 h sedentary screen time; 3 h/day physical activity for 1-4 year olds. AAP: family media plan, co-viewing, screens out of bedrooms.
- **Evidence:** Promising (guideline consensus, mostly observational evidence)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [WHO: Children need to sit less and play more](https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2019-to-grow-up-healthy-children-need-to-sit-less-and-play-more), [AAP: Media and children](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household family media plan + activity challenges.

### Family-based healthy weight
- **Use:** Preventing childhood obesity
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: diet and activity interventions modestly reduce BMI in children, especially activity-focused programmes in schools for 6-12 year olds; whole-family approach recommended.
- **Evidence:** Proven (modest effect)
- **Safety:** Avoid weight stigma; focus on habits.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: preventing obesity in children](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001871)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner family mode; no child weight-shaming UI.

### Child sleep targets
- **Use:** Bedtimes by age
- **What it is / how it's used:** Infants 12-16 h, toddlers 11-14 h, preschool 10-13 h, 6-12 years 9-12 h, teens 8-10 h (incl. naps for under 6).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [CDC: How much sleep do I need?](https://www.Cdc.Gov/sleep/about_sleep/how_much_sleep.html)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household bedtime calculator per child.

## Older relatives and household safety

### Falls prevention at home
- **Use:** Older family members
- **What it is / how it's used:** Strength and balance exercise, medication review, eye tests, remove trip hazards, good lighting, grab rails, sensible footwear; multifactorial assessment after a fall.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Falls prevention](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/falls/prevention/), [NICE NG249 Falls](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng249)
- **HealthHub fit:** Elder-care home safety checklist.

### Early dementia signs
- **Use:** Memory concerns in a relative
- **What it is / how it's used:** Memory loss affecting daily life, difficulty concentrating, confusion about time/place, trouble finding words, mood changes; see a GP early (other treatable causes such as thyroid, B12, depression, infection).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Sudden confusion (delirium) needs urgent assessment.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Symptoms of dementia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dementia/symptoms-and-diagnosis/symptoms/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Carer journal to note examples to share with GP.

### Carbon monoxide safety
- **Use:** Household safety
- **What it is / how it's used:** Fit audible CO alarms, service boilers/stoves yearly; symptoms (headache, dizziness, nausea) that improve away from home and affect several household members are a warning sign.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Suspected CO: get out, fresh air, emergency help.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Carbon monoxide poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carbon-monoxide-poisoning/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household maintenance reminders (alarm battery test, boiler service).

## Red flags (emergency)

### Seriously ill child warning signs
- **Use:** Knowing when to call emergency services
- **What it is / how it's used:** Call 999/112: rash that doesn't fade under a glass, stiff neck, fits, unusually drowsy/floppy/hard to wake, breathing fast/grunting/pauses, blue/grey/blotchy skin, weak high-pitched cry, cold hands/feet with fever, signs of severe dehydration. Sepsis and meningitis can progress in hours.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Meningitis symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/meningitis/symptoms/), [NHS: Sepsis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/), [NICE NG143](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng143)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household illness mode shows these signs prominently with a one-tap call button.
