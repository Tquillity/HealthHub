# Respiratory and immune health: evidence-based science

What Cochrane, NICE, CDC and NHS say about preventing and easing colds, coughs, flu, sore throats, allergies and asthma, including popular remedies that do not work.

## Prevention

### Annual flu vaccination
- **Use:** Preventing influenza and its complications
- **What it is / how it's used:** CDC: everyone 6 months and older every season, with rare exceptions, ideally by end of October. NHS offers it free to over-65s, pregnant women, young children and at-risk groups. Protection wanes, so yearly.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Mild sore arm/fever common; severe allergy to a previous dose is a contraindication.
- **Sources:** [CDC: Key facts about seasonal flu vaccine](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/prevent/keyfacts.htm), [NHS: Flu](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/flu/), [NHS: Vaccinations and when to have them](https://www.nhs.uk/vaccinations/nhs-vaccinations-and-when-to-have-them/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household: per-member vaccine record with autumn reminders.

### Hand hygiene
- **Use:** Reducing spread of colds and other respiratory infections
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane 2023 (78 trials): hand hygiene programmes reduce acute respiratory illness by ~14% relative to no intervention. Effect on lab-confirmed flu alone not significant. Mask evidence uncertain.
- **Evidence:** Proven (modest effect)
- **Safety:** Low risk; frequent alcohol gel can dry skin (moisturise).
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: physical interventions to reduce spread of respiratory viruses](https://www.cochrane.org/CD006207), [PubMed abstract 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36715243/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household "illness mode": when one member is sick, show hygiene routine for everyone.

### Regular moderate exercise
- **Use:** Fewer upper respiratory infections
- **What it is / how it's used:** Observational and some trial data suggest regular moderate activity lowers URTI risk ("J-curve"); very prolonged intense exertion may transiently raise it.
- **Evidence:** Promising
- **Safety:** Low risk; avoid strenuous exercise with fever ("below the neck" symptoms).
- **Sources:** [Nieman 1994 review (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8164529/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Fitness tracker insight; "rest day" suggestion when fever is logged.

### Vitamin D for preventing respiratory infections
- **Use:** Fewer colds/chest infections
- **What it is / how it's used:** 2021 meta-analysis (37 RCTs) showed a small protective effect, mainly with daily 400-1000 IU dosing; a 2024/25 update with more trials found no significant overall effect. NHS advises 10 micrograms/day in autumn/winter for bone health anyway.
- **Evidence:** Promising (small or no effect for infections)
- **Safety:** Do not exceed 100 micrograms (4,000 IU)/day for adults without medical advice.
- **Sources:** [Jolliffe et al. 2021 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33798465/), [Jolliffe et al. 2025 update (LSHTM)](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4677068/1/Jolliffe-etal-2025-Vitamin-d-supplementation-to-prevent.pdf), [NHS: Vitamin D](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Seasonal reminder (October-March) in routines, framed as bone health.

## Colds and coughs

### Common cold self-care
- **Use:** Easing cold symptoms
- **What it is / how it's used:** Rest, fluids, paracetamol or ibuprofen for aches/fever, warm drinks. Colds usually last 1-2 weeks; antibiotics do not help.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline self-care)
- **Safety:** Check combined cold remedies don't double up paracetamol; decongestants not for young children or some heart/BP conditions.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Common cold](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/common-cold/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library "cold" card with day-by-day expected course.

### Honey for acute cough
- **Use:** Cough in people over 1 year
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE NG120: honey may give some relief; in adults it reduced bothersome cough ~2 points on a 7-point scale vs placebo (moderate quality). A teaspoon in warm water/lemon.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline-endorsed, modest effect)
- **Safety:** Never under 1 year (infant botulism); sugar/tooth decay.
- **Sources:** [NICE NG120 summary of evidence](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng120/chapter/summary-of-the-evidence), [NHS: Cough](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cough/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipe: "honey and lemon" drink linked from the cough remedy card, with age check.

### Zinc lozenges at cold onset
- **Use:** Shortening colds
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane 2024 (34 trials): little or no prevention effect; possible shorter duration but very uncertain. Independent re-analysis of lozenge trials reports ~33-37% shorter colds when started within 24 h.
- **Evidence:** Promising (contested)
- **Safety:** Bad taste, nausea; avoid zinc nasal sprays (loss of smell); long-term high doses cause copper deficiency.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Zinc for the common cold 2024](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD014914.pub2/information), [Hemilä & Chalker critique (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11521859/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library with "evidence contested" badge.

### Vitamin C for colds
- **Use:** Preventing/treating colds
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: regular 1 g+/day does not reduce how many colds the general population gets; slightly shorter colds (~8% adults); starting after symptoms begin shows no consistent benefit. Exception: halves cold risk in people under heavy physical stress (marathon runners, soldiers in cold).
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (for general prevention and for treatment once ill)
- **Safety:** Low risk; high doses can cause GI upset and kidney stones in susceptible people.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Vitamin C for the common cold](https://www.cochrane.org/CD000980)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check card.

### Echinacea
- **Use:** Preventing/treating colds
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane 2014 (24 trials): no individual prevention trial was significant; treatment evidence weak; products vary greatly.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (weak/no benefit)
- **Safety:** Allergic reactions possible (daisy family).
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Echinacea for the common cold](https://www.cochrane.org/CD000530/ARI_echinacea-for-preventing-and-treating-the-common-cold)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check card.

### Steam inhalation
- **Use:** Blocked nose with a cold
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: no clear benefit or harm from heated humidified air (low quality). NHS advises against hot-water steam for children due to scald risk.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (no proven benefit; scald risk)
- **Safety:** Scalds, especially children.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Heated, humidified air for the common cold](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001728/ARI_heated-humidified-air-common-cold)
- **HealthHub fit:** Child-safety warning in the remedy library.

## Sore throat, sinusitis and allergies

### Sore throat self-care
- **Use:** Sore throat
- **What it is / how it's used:** Usually viral, better within a week. Paracetamol/ibuprofen, fluids, cool soft foods, warm salt-water gargles (adults). Clinicians use FeverPAIN/Centor to decide antibiotics; low scores should not get them.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** See red flags below.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Sore throat](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sore-throat/), [NICE NG84](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng84/chapter/recommendations)
- **HealthHub fit:** Symptom diary with day counter; "see GP after 7 days" nudge.

### Saline nasal irrigation
- **Use:** Chronic sinusitis, hay fever
- **What it is / how it's used:** Large-volume (~150 mL) saline rinse daily. Cochrane: may help chronic rhinosinusitis (hypertonic) and allergic rhinitis symptoms in adults and children, with minimal harm.
- **Evidence:** Promising
- **Safety:** Use sterile, boiled-and-cooled or distilled water only (tap water has rare amoebic infection risk); clean device.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: saline irrigation for chronic rhinosinusitis](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD011995_saline-irrigation-chronic-rhinosinusitis), [Cochrane: saline for allergic rhinitis](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012597)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine template for hay-fever season.

### Hay fever management
- **Use:** Seasonal allergic rhinitis
- **What it is / how it's used:** Check pollen forecasts, shower/change clothes after being outside, wraparound sunglasses, petroleum jelly around nostrils; non-drowsy antihistamines and steroid nasal sprays from pharmacy (start sprays before season).
- **Evidence:** Proven (medications); avoidance tips are Traditional / anecdotal but low risk
- **Safety:** Older antihistamines cause drowsiness.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Hay fever](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hay-fever/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Dashboard: pollen data + symptom log; routine reminder to start nasal spray 2 weeks before season.

## Asthma

### Supported self-management + written action plan
- **Use:** Adults and children with asthma
- **What it is / how it's used:** Education + self-monitoring (symptoms or peak flow) + regular review + written action plan reduces hospital admissions (RR ~0.64). Action plan alone without support is less effective.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Must be agreed with the clinician; don't change medicines without advice.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: self-management education for adults with asthma](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001117), [NIHR: supported self-management](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/asthma-supported-self-management-most-effective-model-of-care), [NHS: Asthma](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/asthma/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals: peak flow log with green/amber/red zones from the user's own plan; inhaler reminders.

## Red flags (see a doctor)

### Respiratory red flags
- **Use:** Knowing when cough/cold needs medical help
- **What it is / how it's used:** Emergency (999/112): severe breathing difficulty, blue/grey lips, drooling or unable to swallow, chest pain, confusion. See GP: cough >3 weeks, coughing up blood, unexplained weight loss, sore throat >1 week, high fever with weakened immunity, asthma reliever needed more than 3 times a week.
- **Evidence:** Proven (NHS/NICE criteria)
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Cough](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cough/), [NHS: Sore throat](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sore-throat/), [NHS: Asthma](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/asthma/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Symptom log rules: day-21 cough prompt; one-tap emergency info.
