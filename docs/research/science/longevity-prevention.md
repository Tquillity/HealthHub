# Longevity and prevention: evidence-based science

Screening schedules, preventive checks, useful lab tests and the supplements that do and don't extend healthy life, drawn from USPSTF, NHS and large trials.

## Screening calendar

### Bowel (colorectal) cancer screening
- **Use:** Preventing and detecting bowel cancer early
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: ages 45-75 (stool FIT yearly, or colonoscopy every 10 years, among options); NHS: home FIT kit every 2 years for ages 50-74 (being extended). Sweden is rolling out screening from 60.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Positive FIT needs colonoscopy; symptoms should be checked even if screening was normal.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Colorectal cancer screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening), [NHS: Bowel cancer screening](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bowel-cancer-screening/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household screening calendar auto-populated from age, sex and country.

### Lung cancer screening for smokers
- **Use:** Early lung cancer detection
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: annual low-dose CT for adults 50-80 with 20 pack-year history who smoke or quit within 15 years. NHS runs targeted lung health checks for 55-74 ever-smokers.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** False positives, radiation; quitting smoking matters most.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Lung cancer screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/lung-cancer-screening)
- **HealthHub fit:** Pack-year calculator in profile -> eligibility hint.

### Osteoporosis screening
- **Use:** Preventing fractures
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: bone density screening for women 65+ and younger postmenopausal women at increased risk. Risk factors: early menopause, steroid use, low BMI, smoking, family history of hip fracture.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Osteoporosis screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/osteoporosis-screening), [NHS: Osteoporosis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/osteoporosis/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Risk checklist; DEXA results in lab results.

### NHS Health Check (midlife MOT)
- **Use:** Heart, stroke, kidney, diabetes and dementia risk at 40-74
- **What it is / how it's used:** Every 5 years: BP, cholesterol, BMI, HbA1c if indicated, alcohol and activity questions, 10-year CVD risk.
- **Evidence:** Proven (components are guideline-backed)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS Health Check](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/nhs-health-check/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Template for a self-run "annual check" combining vitals and labs.

### One-off infection screening
- **Use:** Hidden infections with effective treatment
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: hepatitis C test once for all adults 18-79; HIV test at least once for ages 15-65 (more often if at risk).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Hepatitis C screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/hepatitis-c-screening), [USPSTF: HIV screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/human-immunodeficiency-virus-hiv-infection-screening)
- **HealthHub fit:** Checklist item "once in a lifetime tests".

### Eye and hearing checks
- **Use:** Detecting glaucoma, macular degeneration, hearing loss
- **What it is / how it's used:** Sight test every 2 years (yearly over 70, with diabetes, or family history of glaucoma 40+). Hearing loss is common and treatable; hearing aids improve quality of life and untreated loss is a modifiable dementia risk factor. (USPSTF finds insufficient evidence for screening asymptomatic adults, but symptoms warrant testing.)
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Sudden hearing or vision loss is urgent.
- **Sources:** [NHS: How often can I have a free sight test](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/opticians/how-often-can-i-have-a-free-nhs-eye-test/), [NHS: Hearing loss](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hearing-loss/), [USPSTF: Hearing loss screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/hearing-loss-in-older-adults-screening)
- **HealthHub fit:** Reminders per household member.

## Supplements: what the big trials show

### Vitamin D in autumn and winter
- **Use:** Bone and muscle health
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS: 10 micrograms (400 IU) daily Oct-March for everyone; year-round for those with little sun exposure or darker skin.
- **Evidence:** Proven (for preventing deficiency)
- **Safety:** Max 100 micrograms/day adults.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Vitamin D](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Seasonal routine reminder.

### High-dose vitamin D for cancer, heart disease or fractures
- **Use:** "Longevity" supplementation in healthy adults
- **What it is / how it's used:** VITAL (25,871 adults, 2,000 IU/day): no reduction in cancer or major cardiovascular events. USPSTF recommends against low-dose vitamin D + calcium to prevent fractures in community-dwelling postmenopausal women and finds no benefit from routine vitamin D deficiency screening.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (for these goals in replete adults)
- **Safety:** Calcium + D slightly increases kidney stones.
- **Sources:** [VITAL vitamin D NEJM 2019 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415629/), [USPSTF: Vitamin D/calcium for fractures](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vitamin-d-calcium-or-combined-supplementation-for-the-primary-prevention-of-fractures-in-adults-preventive-medication), [USPSTF: Vitamin D deficiency screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vitamin-d-deficiency-screening)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement log evidence tags.

### Antioxidant pills (beta-carotene, vitamin E, A)
- **Use:** Anti-ageing / cancer prevention
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane (78 trials, 296,000 people): no mortality benefit; beta-carotene and possibly vitamin E and high-dose vitamin A increase mortality.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful
- **Safety:** Beta-carotene raises lung cancer risk in smokers.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Antioxidant supplements for mortality](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007176), [USPSTF: Vitamin supplementation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vitamin-supplementation-to-prevent-cvd-and-cancer-preventive-medication)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check card.

### Green tea extract for cancer prevention
- **Use:** Cancer prevention
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: evidence is conflicting and insufficient to recommend green tea for cancer prevention.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (unproven; concentrated extracts linked to liver injury)
- **Safety:** Liver toxicity with high-dose extracts; drinking tea is fine.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Green tea for cancer prevention](https://www.cochrane.org/CD005004)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check card.

## Useful lab tests and biomarkers

### Core labs worth tracking
- **Use:** Monitoring preventable risk over time
- **What it is / how it's used:** Guideline-backed: blood pressure, lipid panel (LDL, non-HDL), HbA1c, kidney function (eGFR) and urine albumin when hypertensive/diabetic, BMI/waist. Symptom-driven: ferritin/Hb (fatigue, heavy periods), B12 (vegans, metformin, over 60s), TSH (fatigue, weight change).
- **Evidence:** Proven (for indicated tests); routine "full-body" panels in healthy people not shown to improve outcomes
- **Safety:** Over-testing produces false positives and anxiety.
- **Sources:** [NHS Health Check](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/nhs-health-check/), [NHS: B12 or folate deficiency anaemia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamin-b12-or-folate-deficiency-anaemia/), [NHS: Underactive thyroid](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/underactive-thyroid-hypothyroidism/), [NHS: Iron deficiency anaemia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/iron-deficiency-anaemia/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results feature with reference ranges, trend lines and "why this test" explainer.

### Cardiorespiratory fitness as a longevity biomarker
- **Use:** Predicting mortality
- **What it is / how it's used:** Higher fitness is linked to lower mortality across all levels with no upper limit; improving fitness from the lowest category gives the biggest gains.
- **Evidence:** Proven (large cohort)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Mandsager et al. JAMA Netw Open 2018 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30646252/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals: annual fitness test (VO2max estimate, 6-min walk) alongside labs.

## Lifestyle foundations

### The big modifiable risks
- **Use:** Healthy ageing
- **What it is / how it's used:** Not smoking, regular activity (incl. strength), healthy diet, limited alcohol, healthy weight, good sleep and social connection are the strongest lifestyle levers; WHO attributes most noncommunicable disease deaths to tobacco, inactivity, harmful alcohol and unhealthy diet.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [WHO: Physical activity](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity), [WHO: Healthy diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet), [USPSTF: Unhealthy alcohol use](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/unhealthy-alcohol-use-in-adolescents-and-adults-screening-and-behavioral-counseling-interventions)
- **HealthHub fit:** Dashboard "foundations score" across sleep, activity, diet, alcohol, smoking, social.

### Healthy diet and activity counselling for people at CVD risk
- **Use:** Adults with hypertension, high cholesterol, etc.
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: offer intensive behavioural counselling (multiple sessions) on diet and activity to adults with cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Healthy diet and physical activity counselling](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/healthy-diet-and-physical-activity-counseling-adults-with-high-risk-of-cvd)
- **HealthHub fit:** Justifies in-app coaching programmes (multi-week, goal-based).

## Red flags

### Symptoms not to wait for screening
- **Use:** General cancer and serious disease warning signs
- **What it is / how it's used:** Unexplained weight loss, persistent tiredness, lumps, unexplained bleeding, persistent cough or hoarseness (3+ weeks), change in bowel/bladder habit, a sore that won't heal, changing mole: see a GP regardless of screening status.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Bowel cancer symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bowel-cancer/symptoms/), [NHS: Tiredness and fatigue](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/tiredness-and-fatigue/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Symptom journal "3-week rule" reminders.
