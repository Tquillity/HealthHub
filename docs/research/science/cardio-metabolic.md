# Cardio-metabolic health: evidence-based science

Blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar and weight: proven lifestyle measures, home monitoring, screening and the emergency signs every household should know.

## Blood pressure

### Home blood pressure monitoring
- **Use:** Detecting and managing high blood pressure
- **What it is / how it's used:** Validated upper-arm cuff; sit quietly 5 min, back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level; no caffeine/exercise/smoking 30 min before; take 2 readings 1 min apart, morning and evening, for ~7 days and average. Home average of 135/85 or above is high (ESH/NICE). ESH 2023 endorses home monitoring for diagnosis and follow-up.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk. Single very high reading (180/120 or above) with chest pain, breathlessness, vision change or weakness is an emergency.
- **Sources:** [AHA: Monitoring your blood pressure at home](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home), [NICE NG136 Hypertension](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136), [2023 ESH guidelines (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37345492/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals: guided 7-day BP protocol that averages readings (discarding day 1) and classifies the result.

### DASH eating pattern + lower sodium
- **Use:** Lowering blood pressure
- **What it is / how it's used:** Diet rich in fruit, vegetables, low-fat dairy, wholegrains, nuts and pulses, low in saturated fat and sugar. In DASH-Sodium, DASH plus low sodium lowered systolic BP by ~9 mmHg (more in people with hypertension), with effects within 2 weeks.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; high-potassium diets need care in kidney disease.
- **Sources:** [DASH-Sodium trial (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11136953/), [NHLBI: DASH eating plan](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/education/dash-eating-plan)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner "DASH week" template; recipes tagged DASH-friendly.

### Potassium-enriched salt substitute
- **Use:** Lowering stroke and cardiovascular risk
- **What it is / how it's used:** SSaSS trial (21,000 people with prior stroke or older with high BP): 75% sodium chloride / 25% potassium chloride salt reduced stroke (~14%), major cardiovascular events and death vs regular salt.
- **Evidence:** Proven (large RCT)
- **Safety:** Avoid in kidney disease or with potassium-sparing drugs (ACE inhibitors, spironolactone) without medical advice: hyperkalaemia risk.
- **Sources:** [Neal et al. NEJM 2021 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34459569/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Groceries swap suggestion with a kidney/medication safety check.

### Lifestyle package for high blood pressure
- **Use:** Prevention and first-line treatment of hypertension
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE/NHS: lose weight if overweight, regular aerobic exercise, cut salt, limit alcohol (14 units/week max), stop smoking, limit caffeine. Each can lower BP by several mmHg; combined effects add up.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: High blood pressure](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/high-blood-pressure-hypertension/), [NICE NG136](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136)
- **HealthHub fit:** Dashboard: BP trend next to weight, steps, alcohol and salt logs.

### Blood pressure screening schedule
- **Use:** Catching silent hypertension
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: screen all adults 18+; yearly from 40 or if at increased risk, every 3-5 years for 18-39 at normal risk; confirm outside the clinic. NHS Health Check every 5 years for ages 40-74.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Hypertension screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/hypertension-in-adults-screening), [NHS Health Check](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/nhs-health-check/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household reminders by age.

## Blood sugar and diabetes prevention

### Intensive lifestyle programme for prediabetes
- **Use:** Preventing type 2 diabetes
- **What it is / how it's used:** Diabetes Prevention Program: aim for 7% weight loss and 150 min/week moderate activity; reduced diabetes incidence by 58% vs placebo (31% for metformin). Benefits persist long term.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [DPP NEJM 2002 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/), [NIDDK: Diabetes Prevention Program](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/about-niddk/research-areas/diabetes/diabetes-prevention-program-dpp)
- **HealthHub fit:** A 16-week "prevent diabetes" programme combining weight, activity and meal planner goals.

### Diabetes screening
- **Use:** Finding prediabetes and type 2 diabetes early
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: screen adults 35-70 who are overweight or obese (HbA1c, fasting glucose or OGTT), every 3 years if normal.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Prediabetes and type 2 diabetes screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results: HbA1c storage with ranges (normal <42 mmol/mol / 6.0%; prediabetes 42-47; diabetes 48+).

### Type 2 diabetes remission through weight loss
- **Use:** Recently diagnosed type 2 diabetes with overweight
- **What it is / how it's used:** DiRECT: total diet replacement (~850 kcal/day for 12-20 weeks) then food reintroduction in primary care; 46% in remission at 1 year, strongly linked to amount of weight lost (86% with 15 kg+).
- **Evidence:** Proven (RCT; now NHS programme)
- **Safety:** Must be medically supervised (diabetes and BP medicines need adjusting).
- **Sources:** [DiRECT trial Lancet 2018 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29221645/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Content only; weight tracker supports a clinician-led plan.

### Diabetes warning symptoms
- **Use:** Spotting undiagnosed diabetes
- **What it is / how it's used:** Peeing more (especially at night), thirst, tiredness, unexplained weight loss, genital itching/thrush, slow-healing cuts, blurred vision.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** In children, rapid onset with vomiting/drowsiness may be type 1 with ketoacidosis: same-day care.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Type 2 diabetes symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/type-2-diabetes/symptoms/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Symptom checklist in the remedy library pointing to a GP blood test.

## Cholesterol and heart risk

### Replace saturated fat with unsaturated fat
- **Use:** Lowering LDL cholesterol and cardiovascular events
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: cutting saturated fat for 2+ years reduces combined cardiovascular events by ~17%, most when replaced by polyunsaturated fat (vegetable oils, nuts, oily fish) rather than refined carbs.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: reducing saturated fat](https://www.cochrane.org/CD011737), [NHS: High cholesterol](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/high-cholesterol/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: saturated fat per portion; swap suggestions (butter -> rapeseed oil).

### Cardiovascular risk assessment and statins
- **Use:** Primary prevention of heart attack and stroke
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF: statin for adults 40-75 with 1+ risk factor and 10-year risk 10%+; NICE CG189 suggests offering at 10% QRISK3. Cholesterol test is part of NHS Health Check.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Muscle aches common but rarely caused by statin; discuss with GP.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Statin use](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/statin-use-in-adults-preventive-medication), [NICE: Cardiovascular disease risk](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg189)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results: lipid panel storage; prompt to ask for a risk score after 40.

### Daily aspirin for primary prevention
- **Use:** "Preventing heart attacks" in healthy people
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF 2022: do not start low-dose aspirin for primary prevention at 60+; at 40-59 only an individual decision with 10%+ risk. Bleeding risk often outweighs benefit.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (for most people without heart disease)
- **Safety:** GI and brain bleeding. People already prescribed aspirin after a heart attack/stroke should not stop without advice.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Aspirin](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/aspirin-to-prevent-cardiovascular-disease-preventive-medication)
- **HealthHub fit:** Medication log warning card.

### Fish oil (omega-3) capsules
- **Use:** Preventing heart disease
- **What it is / how it's used:** VITAL (25,871 adults): 1 g/day marine omega-3 did not reduce major cardiovascular events or cancer. Eating oily fish remains recommended.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (for primary prevention in the general population)
- **Safety:** High doses linked to higher atrial fibrillation risk; bleeding with anticoagulants.
- **Sources:** [VITAL omega-3 NEJM 2019 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415637/), [NCCIH: Omega-3 supplements](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/omega3-supplements-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check; meal planner suggests 1 oily fish meal/week instead.

### Cinnamon and garlic supplements
- **Use:** Blood sugar (cinnamon), blood pressure/cholesterol (garlic)
- **What it is / how it's used:** NCCIH: studies are inconsistent; no clear evidence cinnamon helps any health condition; garlic may modestly lower BP and cholesterol but results vary.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (cinnamon); Promising (garlic, small effect)
- **Safety:** Cassia cinnamon contains coumarin (liver); garlic increases bleeding risk with anticoagulants.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Cinnamon](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/cinnamon), [NCCIH: Garlic](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library entries.

## Weight

### Weight and waist measurement
- **Use:** Tracking weight-related risk
- **What it is / how it's used:** BMI 18.5-24.9 healthy (lower cut-offs for South Asian, Chinese, Black African/Caribbean backgrounds: 23/27.5); waist-to-height ratio under 0.5 ("keep your waist to less than half your height"). Losing 5-10% of weight improves BP, glucose and lipids.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** BMI misclassifies muscular people; avoid weight focus for those with eating disorder history.
- **Sources:** [NHS: BMI calculator](https://www.nhs.uk/health-assessment-tools/calculate-your-body-mass-index/), [NHS: Obesity](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/obesity/), [WHO: Obesity and overweight](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/obesity-and-overweight)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals: weight + waist with waist-to-height ratio; optional "hide weight" mode.

## Habits

### Stop smoking (with support)
- **Use:** Largest single cardiovascular risk reduction
- **What it is / how it's used:** Combining behavioural support with medication (varenicline, combination NRT) is most effective; Cochrane finds nicotine e-cigarettes help more people quit than NRT.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** E-cigarettes not risk-free; not for non-smokers.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Tobacco cessation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/tobacco-use-in-adults-and-pregnant-women-counseling-and-interventions), [Cochrane: e-cigarettes for quitting](https://www.cochrane.org/CD010216), [NHS Better Health: Quit smoking](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/quit-smoking/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine: quit-date countdown, money saved, craving journal.

### Alcohol limits
- **Use:** BP, heart rhythm, liver, cancer risk
- **What it is / how it's used:** UK low-risk guideline: no more than 14 units/week spread over 3+ days with drink-free days; less is better.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** No safe level for cancer risk; pregnancy: none.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Calculating alcohol units](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/alcohol-advice/calculating-alcohol-units/), [NHS Better Health: Drink less](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/drink-less/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Tracker: unit calculator from drink type/size.

## Red flags (emergency)

### Stroke (FAST) and heart attack signs
- **Use:** Emergency recognition for the whole household
- **What it is / how it's used:** Stroke FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech slurred, Time to call 999/112. Heart attack: chest pain/pressure spreading to arm, jaw, neck or back, breathlessness, sweating, nausea (women and diabetics may have subtler symptoms). Irregular fast pulse with dizziness or breathlessness: possible atrial fibrillation, see GP promptly.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Call emergency services; do not drive yourself.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Stroke symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/), [NHS: Heart attack symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/symptoms/), [NHS: Atrial fibrillation](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/atrial-fibrillation/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Always-available emergency card; BP readings of 180/120+ trigger a warning screen.
