# Sleep: evidence-based science

What clinical guidelines and systematic reviews say helps adults and children sleep better, plus popular aids that do not hold up and red flags worth surfacing.

## Baseline targets

### Age-based sleep duration targets
- **Use:** Knowing how much sleep each household member needs
- **What it is / how it's used:** Consensus targets (AASM/Sleep Research Society, adopted by CDC): adults 7+ hours; teens 13-17: 8-10 h; school age 6-12: 9-12 h; preschool 3-5: 10-13 h incl. naps; toddlers 1-2: 11-14 h; infants 4-12 months: 12-16 h; newborns 14-17 h.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk. Consistently needing much more than this, or feeling unrefreshed despite enough hours, is worth discussing with a doctor.
- **Sources:** [CDC: How Much Sleep Do I Need?](https://www.Cdc.Gov/sleep/about_sleep/how_much_sleep.html), [AASM: How much sleep do I really need?](https://sleepeducation.org/how-much-sleep-do-i-really-need/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Profile / household: set a per-person sleep target from date of birth and show it on the dashboard.

### Regular sleep-wake schedule
- **Use:** Better sleep quality, fewer insomnia symptoms
- **What it is / how it's used:** Same wake time every day (including weekends) and going to bed only when sleepy. Core plank of NHS self-help and of CBT-I.
- **Evidence:** Proven (as part of CBT-I; standalone "sleep hygiene" alone is weaker)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Insomnia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/), [NHS Every Mind Matters: how to fall asleep faster](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/how-to-fall-asleep-faster-and-sleep-better/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: "anchor wake time" routine with a streak and a weekend-drift warning.

## Falling asleep / chronic insomnia

### Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I)
- **Use:** Chronic insomnia (trouble sleeping 3+ nights/week for 3+ months)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Structured 4-8 week programme: sleep diary, stimulus control, sleep restriction, cognitive work on sleep worries, relaxation. Can be delivered face to face, in groups, by phone, web/app or self-help book. ACP recommends it as first-line for all adults with chronic insomnia, before pills.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; sleep restriction can cause temporary daytime sleepiness (care with driving; caution in bipolar disorder or epilepsy, discuss with a clinician).
- **Sources:** [ACP guideline (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27136449/), [ACP press release](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/acp-recommends-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-as-initial-treatment-for-chronic-insomnia)
- **HealthHub fit:** A guided 6-week CBT-I programme built on the journal + sleep diary; the single highest-value sleep feature.

### Digital / self-guided CBT-I
- **Use:** Insomnia when therapist access is limited
- **What it is / how it's used:** Apps and web programmes delivering CBT-I. Meta-analyses (29 RCTs, ~9,500 people) show clear benefit over controls; therapist-assisted versions perform somewhat better than fully automated ones. Also reduces depressive symptoms.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Fully automated dCBT-I meta-analysis 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11903857), [dCBT-I and depression meta-analysis (PeerJ)](https://peerj.com/articles/16137)
- **HealthHub fit:** Justifies building CBT-I in-app; add optional "share with clinician" export.

### Sleep diary
- **Use:** Understanding insomnia patterns; required input for CBT-I
- **What it is / how it's used:** Log bedtime, time to fall asleep, awakenings, wake time, rise time, naps, caffeine/alcohol. Used to calculate sleep efficiency (time asleep / time in bed); restriction aims for ~85%+.
- **Evidence:** Proven (core CBT-I component)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Insomnia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/), [ACP guideline (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27136449/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Tracker: 30-second morning check-in that auto-computes sleep efficiency and suggests a time-in-bed window.

### Stimulus control ("get up if you can't sleep")
- **Use:** Lying awake in bed
- **What it is / how it's used:** Use bed only for sleep/sex; if awake for roughly 15-20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light, return when sleepy.
- **Evidence:** Proven (CBT-I component)
- **Safety:** Low risk; fall risk for frail older adults getting up at night (use night lights).
- **Sources:** [ACP guideline (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27136449/), [NHS: Insomnia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library card + a "can't sleep" night-mode screen with a dim, calm activity list.

### Worry list / "to-do before bed"
- **Use:** Racing thoughts at bedtime
- **What it is / how it's used:** Write tomorrow's tasks and worries down earlier in the evening so they are "parked".
- **Evidence:** Promising (recommended by NHS; small trials)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS Every Mind Matters: how to fall asleep faster](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/how-to-fall-asleep-faster-and-sleep-better/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal prompt scheduled 1-2 h before bedtime.

### Caffeine cut-off
- **Use:** Difficulty falling or staying asleep
- **What it is / how it's used:** 400 mg caffeine taken 6 hours before bed cut objectively measured sleep by over an hour, and people did not notice. Avoid substantial caffeine at least 6 hours before bed.
- **Evidence:** Promising (one well-designed small study, consistent with pharmacology)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Drake et al. 2013 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3805807), [AASM summary](https://aasm.org/caffeine-in-late-afternoon-early-evening-can-disrupt-sleep/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Tracker: log coffee/tea; show a "caffeine cut-off" time computed from the user's bedtime.

### Alcohol is not a sleep aid
- **Use:** People using a "nightcap" to fall asleep
- **What it is / how it's used:** Alcohol shortens time to fall asleep but fragments the second half of the night and reduces REM sleep at moderate-high doses; the effect does not wear off with nightly use.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (as a sleep aid)
- **Safety:** Regular use worsens sleep and raises dependence risk.
- **Sources:** [Ebrahim et al. 2013 review (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347102/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Insight: correlate logged drinks with next-morning sleep rating in the journal.

## Circadian rhythm, jet lag and shift work

### Morning daylight / bright light
- **Use:** Shifting sleep earlier, circadian rhythm disorders, winter sleep problems
- **What it is / how it's used:** Morning outdoor light or a light box (~10,000 lux, 20-30 min). Meta-analysis of light therapy for sleep problems: moderate effect (g ~0.4) for circadian rhythm disorders and insomnia.
- **Evidence:** Promising (Proven for circadian rhythm sleep disorders in clinical populations)
- **Safety:** Low risk; caution with bipolar disorder (can trigger mania) and some eye conditions.
- **Sources:** [Light therapy for sleep problems meta-analysis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26606319/), [Habitual light exposure and sleep review (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6814154/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine: "morning light walk" paired with the step tracker.

### Melatonin for jet lag
- **Use:** Jet lag, especially eastward travel across 5+ time zones
- **What it is / how it's used:** Low dose (0.5-5 mg) near target bedtime at destination for a few days. Reviews support benefit for jet lag; evidence for chronic insomnia is weak and guidelines (AASM, ACP) do not recommend it for that.
- **Evidence:** Promising (jet lag); Disproven for routine chronic insomnia use (insufficient evidence)
- **Safety:** Short-term use appears safe; products vary in actual content; prescription-only in UK/EU in many cases; caution with anticoagulants and epilepsy; avoid gummies being treated as sweets by children.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Melatonin](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know), [NHS: Jet lag](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/jet-lag/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Travel mode: a jet-lag planner that shifts sleep and light timing before a trip.

## Night waking and sleep disorders

### Obstructive sleep apnoea screening (STOP-BANG)
- **Use:** Loud snoring, witnessed breathing pauses, daytime sleepiness
- **What it is / how it's used:** 8 yes/no items (Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnoea, high blood Pressure, BMI >35, Age >50, Neck >40 cm, male Gender). Score 3+ = increased risk; ask a doctor about a (often home) sleep study. NICE NG202 covers diagnosis and CPAP.
- **Evidence:** Proven (validated screening questionnaire; treatment with CPAP guideline-backed)
- **Safety:** Screening only, not a diagnosis. Untreated OSA raises crash, hypertension and cardiovascular risk; drowsy driving is a red flag.
- **Sources:** [AASM: STOP-BANG](https://sleepeducation.org/what-is-the-stop-bang-questionnaire/), [NICE NG202](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng202), [NHS: Sleep apnoea](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals/profile: a self-screen questionnaire that suggests talking to a GP when score is 3+; partner can log snoring.

### Restless legs: check iron
- **Use:** Urge to move legs in the evening, disrupting sleep
- **What it is / how it's used:** Linked to low iron; guidance recommends a ferritin blood test, with oral iron if ferritin is low (targets around 75-100 micrograms/L vary by area). Self-care: reduce caffeine/alcohol, leg massage, moderate exercise, symptom diary.
- **Evidence:** Proven (iron testing and replacement when low)
- **Safety:** Do not self-prescribe iron long term without a blood test: iron overload is harmful.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Restless legs syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/restless-legs-syndrome/), [Harvard Health: iron for restless legs](https://www.health.harvard.edu/diseases-and-conditions/are-you-missing-this-simple-treatment-for-restless-legs)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results: store ferritin; journal symptom diary for RLS.

## Supplements and aids

### Magnesium for insomnia
- **Use:** Falling asleep (older adults)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Meta-analysis of 3 small RCTs (151 older adults): ~17 min faster sleep onset, no significant change in total sleep; low to very low quality evidence.
- **Evidence:** Promising (weak)
- **Safety:** Diarrhoea at higher doses; avoid supplements in kidney disease; interacts with some antibiotics and osteoporosis drugs.
- **Sources:** [Mah & Pitre 2021 meta-analysis (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053283)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library entry clearly labelled "weak evidence".

### Valerian
- **Use:** Insomnia
- **What it is / how it's used:** Herbal extract sold as a sleep aid. A 2024 umbrella review of 8 systematic reviews found no evidence of efficacy for insomnia, though it appears safe; the most rigorous trials show no effect.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (ineffective; safe)
- **Safety:** Generally safe short term; rare liver problems reported; avoid with sedatives/alcohol.
- **Sources:** [Umbrella review 2024 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38359657/), [Bent et al. 2006 meta-analysis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17145239/)
- **HealthHub fit:** "Myth check" card in the remedy library.

### Over-the-counter sleeping pills (antihistamines)
- **Use:** Short-term sleeplessness
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS: pharmacy sleep aids may help for 1-2 weeks but cannot cure insomnia and should not be taken longer.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (for long-term use)
- **Safety:** Next-day drowsiness; anticholinergic effects, confusion and falls in older adults.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Insomnia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Medication log warning when a sleep aid has been logged for more than 14 days.

## Red flags (see a doctor)

### Sleep red flags
- **Use:** Knowing when self-help is not enough
- **What it is / how it's used:** See a GP if insomnia persists for months or habit changes fail, if loud snoring with gasping/pauses or dozing while driving, if leg symptoms disturb sleep most nights, or if sleep problems come with low mood or thoughts of self-harm.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline referral criteria)
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Insomnia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/), [NHS: Sleep apnoea](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-apnoea/), [NHS: Snoring](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/snoring/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Rule-based nudges from the sleep tracker (e.g. 4+ weeks of poor sleep ratings -> "consider talking to your GP").
