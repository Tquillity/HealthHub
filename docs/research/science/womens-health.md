# Women's health: evidence-based science

Menstrual cycle, pregnancy, postpartum and menopause: what guidelines and systematic reviews support, screening schedules and red flags that a cycle tracker could surface.

## Menstrual cycle

### Period pain: NSAIDs, heat and exercise
- **Use:** Dysmenorrhoea
- **What it is / how it's used:** Ibuprofen or naproxen, ideally started at the first sign of pain, are first line; heat pads, light exercise and TENS help; hormonal contraception if needed. See a GP if pain is severe, disrupts daily life or is new after 25.
- **Evidence:** Proven (NSAIDs); Promising (heat, exercise)
- **Safety:** NSAIDs: avoid with stomach ulcers, some asthma, kidney disease, late pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Period pain](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/period-pain/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle tracker: predict likely painful days and prompt "start ibuprofen early / heat pad".

### Supplements for period pain
- **Use:** Dysmenorrhoea
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: no high-quality evidence for any dietary supplement; low-quality evidence hints at ginger, fenugreek, fish oil, vitamin B1, zinc.
- **Evidence:** Promising (weak / low quality)
- **Safety:** Generally low risk at dietary doses.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: dietary supplements for period pain](https://www.cochrane.org/CD002124)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library, labelled "low-quality evidence".

### PMS symptom diary
- **Use:** Premenstrual syndrome / PMDD
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS: keep a symptom diary for at least 2 cycles to confirm timing; regular exercise, balanced diet, enough sleep, stress reduction; see GP for severe symptoms (PMDD), where CBT, hormonal options or SSRIs may help.
- **Evidence:** Proven (diary for diagnosis; CBT/SSRIs for severe PMS)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: PMS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pre-menstrual-syndrome/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle + journal: auto-chart symptoms against cycle day to show premenstrual clustering.

### Heavy periods and iron
- **Use:** Heavy menstrual bleeding, fatigue
- **What it is / how it's used:** Heavy if changing pad/tampon every 1-2 hours, clots bigger than ~2.5 cm, bleeding >7 days, or affecting life. Treatments exist (tranexamic acid, hormonal IUD). Heavy periods are a common cause of iron-deficiency anaemia.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Tiredness, breathlessness, pale skin: ask for a blood test.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Heavy periods](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heavy-periods/), [NHS: Iron deficiency anaemia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/iron-deficiency-anaemia/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle tracker: flow/product-count logging with a "this looks heavy" nudge; lab results for ferritin/Hb.

### Endometriosis and PCOS awareness
- **Use:** Severe pain, irregular periods, fertility issues
- **What it is / how it's used:** Endometriosis: period pain that stops normal activities, pain during/after sex, pain pooing/peeing during period, difficulty conceiving (average diagnostic delay ~8 years). PCOS (NHS now uses the new name PMOS): irregular periods, excess hair, acne, weight gain; weight loss and exercise improve symptoms.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Endometriosis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/endometriosis/), [NHS: PCOS / PMOS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle tracker pattern detection (e.g. cycles >35 days, pain scores 8+) suggesting a GP visit with an exportable summary.

## Fertility and pregnancy

### Folic acid before and in early pregnancy
- **Use:** Preventing neural tube defects
- **What it is / how it's used:** 400 micrograms daily from before conception (or as soon as possible) until 12 weeks; higher 5 mg dose for those at higher risk (diabetes, epilepsy meds, previous NTD, BMI 30+). USPSTF: 0.4-0.8 mg for anyone who could become pregnant.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [USPSTF: Folic acid](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/folic-acid-for-the-prevention-of-neural-tube-defects-preventive-medication), [NHS: B vitamins and folic acid](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-b/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Profile "trying to conceive" mode: daily folic acid reminder.

### Foods to avoid in pregnancy
- **Use:** Food safety in pregnancy
- **What it is / how it's used:** Avoid unpasteurised/mould-ripened soft cheeses, raw/undercooked meat and non-Lion-stamped raw eggs, liver/pâté and vitamin A supplements, high-mercury fish (shark, swordfish, marlin; limit tuna), alcohol; limit caffeine to 200 mg/day.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Foods to avoid in pregnancy](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes/meal planner: "pregnancy-safe" filter and ingredient warnings; caffeine counter.

### Exercise in pregnancy
- **Use:** Healthy pregnancy, lower gestational diabetes risk
- **What it is / how it's used:** ~150 min/week moderate activity if uncomplicated; avoid contact sports, lying flat on the back after ~16 weeks, scuba diving, overheating.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Stop and seek advice with bleeding, dizziness, chest pain, fluid leaking, painful contractions.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Exercise in pregnancy](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/exercise/), [ACOG: Exercise during pregnancy](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/exercise-during-pregnancy)
- **HealthHub fit:** Fitness routines filtered by trimester.

### Omega-3 in pregnancy
- **Use:** Reducing preterm birth
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: omega-3 (mainly DHA) supplementation reduces preterm (<37 weeks) and early preterm birth risk; oily fish 1-2x/week is the food route.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Avoid fish-liver oils (vitamin A).
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: omega-3 during pregnancy](https://www.cochrane.org/CD003402)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner suggestion during pregnancy mode.

### Pregnancy red flags
- **Use:** Urgent pregnancy symptoms
- **What it is / how it's used:** Seek urgent care for: one-sided tummy pain with bleeding, shoulder-tip pain or fainting in early pregnancy (possible ectopic); vaginal bleeding; severe headache, vision problems, sudden swelling of face/hands (possible pre-eclampsia); reduced baby movements; fluid leaking.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Ectopic pregnancy symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ectopic-pregnancy/symptoms/), [NHS: Pregnancy](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pregnancy-and-baby/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Pregnancy mode emergency card; journal keyword triggers.

## Postpartum

### Postnatal depression awareness
- **Use:** Low mood after birth (affects ~1 in 10)
- **What it is / how it's used:** "Baby blues" lasting more than 2 weeks, inability to enjoy things, withdrawal, intrusive thoughts: see GP/health visitor; talking therapies and medication help. Partners can be affected too.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Thoughts of harming self or baby, or confusion/hallucinations (postpartum psychosis) are emergencies.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Postnatal depression](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/post-natal-depression/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household: offer EPDS-style mood check at 2, 6 and 12 weeks after a birth is logged, for both parents.

### Pelvic floor muscle training
- **Use:** Preventing and treating urinary incontinence (pregnancy, postpartum, menopause)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: supervised PFMT cures or improves stress and mixed incontinence; usual programme ~3 sets of 8-12 squeezes daily for at least 3 months.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: PFMT for urinary incontinence in women](https://www.cochrane.org/CD005654), [NHS: Urinary incontinence](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/urinary-incontinence/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine with discreet daily reminders and timer-guided squeeze/hold sets.

## Menopause

### Menopause: HRT and CBT
- **Use:** Hot flushes, night sweats, low mood, sleep problems
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE NG23 (2024 update): HRT is the most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms; CBT is recommended as an option for hot flushes, sleep and mood. Lifestyle: exercise, layers, cut triggers (caffeine, alcohol, spicy food). Vaginal oestrogen for genital symptoms.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** HRT risks depend on type and personal history (breast cancer, clots); individual discussion needed.
- **Sources:** [NICE NG23 Menopause](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23), [NHS: Menopause and perimenopause](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/menopause/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle tracker transitions to "perimenopause mode": hot flush log, symptom score to bring to a GP.

### Black cohosh for hot flushes
- **Use:** Menopausal symptoms
- **What it is / how it's used:** Studies are mixed and largely negative; NCCIH says evidence does not clearly show it relieves symptoms.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (ineffective; liver concerns)
- **Safety:** Rare liver damage reported.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Black cohosh](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/black-cohosh)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check card.

## Infections and screening

### Cranberry for recurrent UTIs
- **Use:** Preventing recurrent urinary tract infections
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane 2023: cranberry products reduce risk of repeat symptomatic UTIs in women with recurrent UTIs, children and after some procedures; not useful for treating an active infection.
- **Evidence:** Proven (prevention in specific groups)
- **Safety:** May interact with warfarin; sugary juices add sugar.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Cranberries for preventing UTIs](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001321), [NHS: UTIs](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/urinary-tract-infections-utis/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library; UTI symptom log.

### Cervical and breast screening schedule
- **Use:** Early cancer detection
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cervical (HPV) screening: UK every 3-5 years age 25-64; USPSTF 21-65. Breast mammography: NHS every 3 years age 50-71; USPSTF every 2 years age 40-74. Breast awareness: report lumps, skin dimpling, nipple changes.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** False positives/overdiagnosis are known harms; informed choice.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Cervical screening](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cervical-screening/), [NHS: Breast screening](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/breast-screening-mammogram/), [USPSTF: Breast cancer screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/breast-cancer-screening), [USPSTF: Cervical cancer screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/cervical-cancer-screening)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household screening calendar per member and country.

### Vaginal thrush self-care
- **Use:** Itching, thick white discharge
- **What it is / how it's used:** Pharmacy antifungals (clotrimazole, fluconazole); avoid perfumed products and douching. See GP if first time, under 16/over 60, pregnant, recurrent (4+ a year) or not improving.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Oral fluconazole not in pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Thrush](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vaginal-thrush/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle-linked symptom log; recurrence counter.
