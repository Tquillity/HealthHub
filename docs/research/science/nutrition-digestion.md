# Nutrition and digestion: evidence-based science

Dietary guidance from WHO, NHS, NICE and Livsmedelsverket, plus the evidence on gut complaints, supplements and red-flag symptoms.

## Everyday healthy eating

### WHO healthy diet targets
- **Use:** A simple, global benchmark for a healthy diet
- **What it is / how it's used:** At least 400 g (5 portions) of fruit and vegetables a day (potatoes excluded); free sugars under 10% of energy (ideally under 5%); salt under 5 g/day (about a teaspoon), iodised; limit saturated fat (<10% energy) and avoid industrial trans fat.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [WHO: Healthy diet fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner: score each planned day against the WHO targets (veg grams, salt, sugar).

### Swedish dietary guidelines ("Hitta ditt sätt")
- **Use:** Locally adapted guidance for Swedish households
- **What it is / how it's used:** Based on Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023: more vegetables, fruit, pulses, wholegrains, fish and nuts; switch to vegetable oils; less red and processed meat (max ~350 g cooked red meat/week), salt, sugar and alcohol; move more. Keyhole (Nyckelhålet) label marks healthier options.
- **Evidence:** Proven (national authority guidance)
- **Safety:** Low risk; separate advice for children, pregnancy and older adults.
- **Sources:** [Livsmedelsverket: Kostråd](https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/matvanor-halsa--miljo/kostrad), [1177: Ha det gott – råd om hälsosamma matvanor](https://www.1177.se/Skane/aktuellt/aktuellt-i-skane/ha-det-gott/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Groceries: tag Keyhole products; weekly red-meat counter in the meal planner.

### NHS Eatwell Guide
- **Use:** Balancing a plate / weekly shop
- **What it is / how it's used:** Visual proportions: about a third fruit and veg, a third starchy wholegrain carbs, plus protein (2 portions fish/week, one oily), some dairy/alternatives, small amounts of unsaturated oils; 6-8 drinks a day.
- **Evidence:** Proven (national guidance)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: The Eatwell Guide](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-guidelines-and-food-labels/the-eatwell-guide/), [NHS: Fish and shellfish](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-types/fish-and-shellfish-nutrition/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: auto-classify recipes into Eatwell groups and show a plate chart.

### Fibre 25-30 g+ per day
- **Use:** Lower risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, bowel cancer; regular bowels
- **What it is / how it's used:** WHO-commissioned meta-analyses (Lancet 2019, 185 cohorts + 58 trials) found greatest risk reduction at 25-29 g/day, with more probably better. Sources: wholegrains, pulses, vegetables, fruit, nuts, seeds. Increase gradually with fluids.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; increase slowly to limit bloating; insoluble fibre (bran) can worsen IBS.
- **Sources:** [Reynolds et al. Lancet 2019 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30638909/), [NHS: How to get more fibre](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/how-to-get-more-fibre-into-your-diet/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes/meal planner: per-recipe fibre grams and a daily fibre bar.

### Mediterranean-style diet
- **Use:** Heart health, overall diet pattern
- **What it is / how it's used:** Vegetables, legumes, wholegrains, fish, nuts, extra-virgin olive oil, moderate dairy, little red meat. PREDIMED (re-analysed after randomisation problems) showed ~30% fewer major cardiovascular events (mainly stroke); Cochrane rates evidence as low-moderate.
- **Evidence:** Promising (strong observational + one large trial with caveats)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane review on Mediterranean diet (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30864165/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: "Mediterranean" tag and weekly adherence score (e.g. MEDAS-style checklist).

### Limit ultra-processed foods
- **Use:** Reducing diet-related disease risk
- **What it is / how it's used:** BMJ 2024 umbrella review (~10 million people): higher UPF intake associated with higher cardiovascular death, common mental disorders and type 2 diabetes (convincing evidence grade), and all-cause mortality. Observational, not proof of cause.
- **Evidence:** Promising (consistent observational evidence)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Lane et al. BMJ 2024 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10899807), [BMJ press release](https://bmjgroup.com/consistent-evidence-links-ultra-processed-food-to-over-30-damaging-health-outcomes/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Groceries: flag likely UPF items; recipes promote home-cooked swaps.

### Salt reduction
- **Use:** Lower blood pressure
- **What it is / how it's used:** Adults max 6 g salt/day (NHS), 5 g (WHO). Most salt is already in bread, cereals, ready meals and processed meat; check labels (>1.5 g salt/100 g = high).
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Salt in your diet](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-types/salt-nutrition/), [DASH-Sodium trial (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11136953/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: salt per portion with traffic-light colour.

## Hydration

### Daily fluid needs
- **Use:** Staying hydrated
- **What it is / how it's used:** EFSA adequate total water intake (food + drinks): 2.0 L/day women, 2.5 L/day men in temperate conditions (+300 mL pregnancy, +700 mL breastfeeding); NHS suggests 6-8 drinks/day. Needs rise with heat, exercise and illness. Pale yellow urine is a practical check.
- **Evidence:** Proven (reference values)
- **Safety:** Excess water intake (hyponatraemia) is a risk in endurance events; some heart/kidney patients have fluid limits.
- **Sources:** [EFSA dietary reference values press release](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sv/press/news/nda100326), [NHS: Dehydration](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Tracker: simple water counter with goal adjusted for sex, pregnancy and logged exercise.

## Constipation, IBS and bloating

### Constipation self-care
- **Use:** Infrequent or hard stools
- **What it is / how it's used:** Increase fibre gradually, drink enough, be active, do not delay going, use a footstool to raise knees. Pharmacist can advise on laxatives (bulk-forming first) if no improvement.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline first-line)
- **Safety:** See a GP if no improvement after ~2 weeks or with red flags.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Constipation](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/constipation/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: bowel habit log (Bristol stool scale) correlated with fibre and water logs.

### IBS: soluble fibre (psyllium/ispaghula, oats)
- **Use:** IBS pain and global symptoms
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE: if fibre is increased, use soluble fibre (ispaghula, oats); avoid insoluble (bran). BSG: start 3-4 g/day and build up.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Take with plenty of water; start low to avoid bloating.
- **Sources:** [NICE CG61 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg61/chapter/1-Recommendations), [BSG IBS guideline 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8231425)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library + groceries suggestion.

### IBS: peppermint oil
- **Use:** IBS pain and spasms
- **What it is / how it's used:** Enteric-coated capsules; BSG says may be effective for global symptoms and pain (low quality evidence).
- **Evidence:** Promising
- **Safety:** Can cause heartburn/reflux.
- **Sources:** [BSG IBS guideline 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8231425)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library.

### Low-FODMAP diet (supervised)
- **Use:** IBS symptoms persisting despite general advice
- **What it is / how it's used:** Short elimination of fermentable carbs then structured reintroduction to find personal triggers; NICE says only with a dietary-trained professional.
- **Evidence:** Promising (NICE rates the trials very low quality, but widely used)
- **Safety:** Risk of unnecessarily restrictive eating and reduced gut bacteria diversity if kept long term; not for people with eating disorders.
- **Sources:** [NICE CG61 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg61/chapter/1-Recommendations), [NHS: IBS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/irritable-bowel-syndrome-ibs/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner "low-FODMAP phase" with a food-symptom journal for reintroduction.

### Food-symptom diary
- **Use:** Bloating, IBS, suspected intolerance
- **What it is / how it's used:** NHS and NICE suggest keeping a food and symptom diary to identify triggers before cutting foods out.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline-recommended practice)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Bloating](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bloating/), [NHS: IBS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/irritable-bowel-syndrome-ibs/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: link symptoms to meals from the meal planner automatically.

### Coeliac disease: test before going gluten-free
- **Use:** Suspected gluten problem (bloating, diarrhoea, anaemia, fatigue)
- **What it is / how it's used:** Blood test while still eating gluten; going gluten-free first can give a false negative.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Undiagnosed coeliac disease causes anaemia, osteoporosis.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Coeliac disease](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/coeliac-disease/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Nudge when user starts a "gluten-free" meal plan: "get tested first".

## Heartburn and probiotics

### Reflux lifestyle measures
- **Use:** Heartburn / acid reflux
- **What it is / how it's used:** Smaller meals, avoid eating 3-4 h before bed, raise the head of the bed (not extra pillows), lose weight if overweight, stop smoking, identify trigger foods/drinks.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline self-care)
- **Safety:** See GP if symptoms several times a week, OTC medicines don't help, or red flags.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Heartburn and acid reflux](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/), [NHS: Indigestion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/indigestion/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner: optional "last meal by" time; journal trigger tracking.

### Probiotics with antibiotics
- **Use:** Preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhoea
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane (2025 update, adults and children): probiotics reduce risk of antibiotic-associated and C. difficile diarrhoea; in children high-dose probiotics NNT ~6.
- **Evidence:** Proven (for this specific use; general "gut health" claims are not established)
- **Safety:** Avoid in severely immunocompromised people or those with central lines.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: probiotics for antibiotic-associated diarrhoea](https://www.cochrane.org/CD006095/INFECT_antibiotic-associated-diarrhoea-probiotics), [Cochrane: paediatric review](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004827.pub5/information)
- **HealthHub fit:** Medication log: when an antibiotic is logged, suggest discussing a probiotic.

## Supplements shown not to help

### Multivitamins / antioxidant pills for disease prevention
- **Use:** "Insurance" against heart disease and cancer
- **What it is / how it's used:** USPSTF 2022: insufficient evidence for multivitamins; recommends against beta-carotene and vitamin E (beta-carotene raises lung cancer risk in smokers). Cochrane: antioxidant supplements do not reduce mortality, some may increase it.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful
- **Safety:** Beta-carotene harmful for smokers; high-dose vitamin E linked to bleeding risk. Targeted supplements (folic acid in pregnancy, vitamin D in winter, B12 for vegans) are exceptions.
- **Sources:** [USPSTF vitamin supplementation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vitamin-supplementation-to-prevent-cvd-and-cancer-preventive-medication), [Cochrane: antioxidant supplements](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007176)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement log that tags each item with its evidence level.

## Red flags (see a doctor)

### Bowel and stomach red flags
- **Use:** Knowing when digestive symptoms need checking
- **What it is / how it's used:** See a GP for: blood in poo or black tarry stools, change in bowel habit for 3+ weeks, unexplained weight loss, persistent tummy pain/lump, difficulty swallowing, vomiting blood, iron-deficiency anaemia, new symptoms over 55. Emergency for heavy non-stop rectal bleeding.
- **Evidence:** Proven (NHS/NICE referral criteria)
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Bowel cancer symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bowel-cancer/symptoms/), [NHS: Heartburn and acid reflux](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: if "blood in stool" or 3+ weeks of changed habit is logged, show a clear "book a GP appointment" banner.
