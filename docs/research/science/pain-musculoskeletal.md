# Pain and musculoskeletal health: evidence-based science

Headache, migraine, back and joint pain, sprains: what NICE and Cochrane support, what doesn't work, and red flags.

## Back and neck pain

### Stay active with low back pain
- **Use:** Acute and chronic low back pain
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE NG59: reassurance, keep moving and continue normal activities as far as possible; most acute back pain improves within weeks. Bed rest delays recovery.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; see red flags.
- **Sources:** [NICE NG59 Low back pain](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59), [NHS: Back pain](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library "back pain" card + gentle movement routine.

### Exercise therapy for chronic back pain
- **Use:** Chronic low back pain
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane (249 trials): exercise reduces pain and improves function compared with no treatment or usual care; no single type clearly best (Pilates, strength, yoga, walking all reasonable). NICE: group exercise programmes.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Exercise for chronic low back pain](https://www.cochrane.org/CD009790), [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: progressive 12-week back programme with pain rating log.

### Medicines for back pain
- **Use:** Pain relief
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE NG59: NSAIDs at lowest dose for shortest time if needed; do not offer paracetamol alone, and avoid opioids for chronic back pain; gabapentinoids and antidepressants not recommended for sciatica/back pain.
- **Evidence:** Proven (paracetamol alone ineffective for back pain)
- **Safety:** NSAID GI/kidney/heart risks.
- **Sources:** [NICE NG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59)
- **HealthHub fit:** Medication log with "paracetamol alone is not recommended for back pain" note.

### Neck pain self-care
- **Use:** Stiff/painful neck
- **What it is / how it's used:** Keep moving gently, paracetamol/ibuprofen, heat, posture breaks; usually improves within weeks. Manipulation/mobilisation gives at most short-term modest benefit (Cochrane).
- **Evidence:** Proven (self-care); Promising (manipulation)
- **Safety:** Rare serious adverse events from neck manipulation.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Neck pain](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/neck-pain/), [Cochrane: Manipulation and mobilisation for neck disorders](https://www.cochrane.org/CD004249)
- **HealthHub fit:** Desk-break routine in the timer.

## Joints

### Exercise for knee and hip osteoarthritis
- **Use:** OA pain and function
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: land-based exercise reduces pain and improves function in knee and hip OA. NICE NG226: therapeutic exercise and weight loss (if overweight) are core treatments for everyone with OA.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk; mild post-exercise soreness is normal.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Exercise for knee OA](https://www.cochrane.org/CD004376), [Cochrane: Exercise for hip OA](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007912), [NICE NG226 Osteoarthritis](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng226)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: OA strength programme; weight tracker link.

### Glucosamine and chondroitin
- **Use:** Osteoarthritis
- **What it is / how it's used:** Large NIH-funded trials show no meaningful benefit over placebo for most people; NICE NG226 says do not offer glucosamine.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (ineffective)
- **Safety:** May interact with warfarin; shellfish-derived products.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Glucosamine and chondroitin](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/glucosamine-and-chondroitin-for-osteoarthritis), [NICE NG226](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng226)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth-check card in supplement log.

### Acupuncture for osteoarthritis
- **Use:** OA pain
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cochrane: benefits over sham are small and may be due to placebo/expectation; NICE NG226 says do not offer acupuncture for OA.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (not clinically meaningful for OA)
- **Safety:** Low risk with qualified practitioner.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Acupuncture for OA](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001977), [NCCIH: Acupuncture](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/acupuncture-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library note.

### Topical NSAIDs for knee/hand OA
- **Use:** Joint pain
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE NG226: offer topical NSAID first for knee OA (and consider for other joints) before oral painkillers; fewer systemic side effects.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Skin irritation; avoid on broken skin.
- **Sources:** [NICE NG226](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng226)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library; medication log.

### Turmeric / curcumin
- **Use:** Joint pain, "inflammation"
- **What it is / how it's used:** Some small studies suggest OA symptom relief but evidence is limited and inconsistent; poorly absorbed.
- **Evidence:** Promising (weak)
- **Safety:** Supplements linked to rare liver injury; interacts with anticoagulants.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Turmeric](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library with safety banner; fine as a recipe spice.

## Injuries

### Sprains and strains (PRICE then movement)
- **Use:** Acute soft tissue injury
- **What it is / how it's used:** First 2-3 days: Protect, Rest, Ice (wrapped, 20 min every 2-3 h), Compression, Elevation; avoid heat, alcohol, running, massage early (HARM). Then gentle movement; most improve in 2 weeks.
- **Evidence:** Proven (guideline self-care)
- **Safety:** Get checked if unable to bear weight, deformity, numbness, severe pain.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)
- **HealthHub fit:** First-aid card with a 72-hour timer and ice reminders.

### Plantar heel pain
- **Use:** Plantar fasciitis
- **What it is / how it's used:** Rest, ice, supportive shoes/insoles, calf and plantar fascia stretches, weight loss; usually resolves within months.
- **Evidence:** Proven (self-care)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NHS: Plantar fasciitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/plantar-fasciitis/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Stretch routine.

## Headache and migraine

### Headache diary and medication-overuse check
- **Use:** Frequent headaches, migraine
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE CG150: keep a diary (frequency, duration, triggers, medicines, menstrual link); medication overuse headache if simple painkillers on 15+ days/month or triptans/combination/opioids on 10+ days/month for 3+ months.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Stopping overused medicines can temporarily worsen headache; plan with GP.
- **Sources:** [NICE CG150 Headaches](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg150), [NHS: Headaches](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/headaches/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Tracker: headache diary with automatic medication-day count and overuse warning; cycle overlay for menstrual migraine.

### Acute migraine treatment
- **Use:** Migraine attacks
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE: triptan plus NSAID or paracetamol as early as possible; anti-sickness medicine if nauseous; rest in a dark room. Opioids not recommended.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Triptans not for people with heart disease/uncontrolled BP.
- **Sources:** [NICE CG150](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg150), [NHS: Migraine](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/migraine/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library; diary logs treatment response.

### Migraine prevention: lifestyle, acupuncture, riboflavin
- **Use:** Frequent migraine
- **What it is / how it's used:** Regular sleep, meals and hydration; aerobic exercise. Cochrane: acupuncture reduces migraine frequency (comparable to prophylactic drugs). NICE: consider a course of up to 10 acupuncture sessions; riboflavin 400 mg/day may help some people.
- **Evidence:** Proven (acupuncture, Cochrane); Promising (riboflavin)
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Acupuncture for preventing migraine](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001218), [NICE CG150](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg150)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine for consistent sleep/meal timing; diary measures change in monthly migraine days.

## Chronic pain

### Chronic primary pain: exercise and psychological therapy
- **Use:** Long-term pain (e.g. fibromyalgia, chronic widespread pain)
- **What it is / how it's used:** NICE NG193: offer supervised group exercise, CBT or ACT, and acupuncture; do not start opioids, paracetamol, NSAIDs, gabapentinoids or benzodiazepines for chronic primary pain. Cochrane overview supports physical activity for chronic pain.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** Low risk
- **Sources:** [NICE NG193 Chronic pain](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng193), [Cochrane overview: physical activity for chronic pain](https://www.cochrane.org/CD011279)
- **HealthHub fit:** Pain + activity + mood dashboard; pacing routine.

## Red flags

### Back pain and headache red flags
- **Use:** When to seek urgent care
- **What it is / how it's used:** Back pain with numbness around genitals/buttocks, loss of bladder/bowel control or weakness in both legs (cauda equina: emergency); back pain with fever, weight loss, history of cancer, after major trauma. Headache: sudden "thunderclap" worst ever, with fever and stiff neck or rash, after head injury, with confusion, weakness, speech or vision problems, new headache over 50 or in pregnancy.
- **Evidence:** Proven
- **Safety:** n/a
- **Sources:** [NHS: Back pain](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/), [NHS: Sciatica](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sciatica/), [NHS: Headaches](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/headaches/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Pain log asks red-flag yes/no questions and shows emergency guidance.
