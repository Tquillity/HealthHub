# Skin, hair and oral: traditional and folk remedies

Traditional skin and wound remedies, from Lapland spruce resin to oatmeal baths and tea tree oil.

## Wounds and ulcers

### Spruce resin salve (kådsalva, pihkavoide)
- **Use:** Wounds, infected cuts, pressure ulcers, cracked skin.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Finnish Lapland and Nordic folk medicine. Norway spruce resin is cooked with fat (butter or lard) into a salve and applied under a dressing. It is now made as a medical product in Finland.
- **Evidence:** Promising. In a randomised trial (37 patients), 92% of pressure ulcers healed with resin salve versus 44% with hydrocolloid dressings. A pilot on difficult surgical wounds found 100% healing. Lab tests show it is active against Gram-positive bacteria, including MRSA. The trials are small and the authors had industry ties.
- **Safety:** Allergy (colophony/resin contact allergy). Do not use on deep wounds without medical care.
- **Sources:** [Sipponen 2008 RCT, pressure ulcers](https://www.pubmed.gov/18284391/), [Sipponen 2012 pilot, surgical wounds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607295/), [Jokinen & Sipponen 2016 review (PMC4827294)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4827294)
- **HealthHub fit:** "Nordic remedies" section in the remedy library. It has an actual evidence story.

### Honey on wounds
- **Use:** Minor wounds and burns.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Ancient Egyptian, Greek and Ayurvedic practice. Medical-grade honey dressings today.
- **Evidence:** Promising / Proven for partial-thickness burns (Cochrane). Unclear for other wounds.
- **Safety:** Use sterile medical honey.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Honey as a topical treatment for wounds](https://www.cochrane.org/CD005083)
- **HealthHub fit:** First-aid kit list.

## Eczema and itchy skin

### Oatmeal bath (havrebad)
- **Use:** Eczema, chickenpox itch, dry skin.
- **What it is / how it's used:** European and North American grandmother remedy. Oats in a sock or muslin bag in a lukewarm bath, or finely ground colloidal oatmeal.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Colloidal oatmeal is anti-itch and helps repair the skin barrier, and studies support oat creams and cleansers alongside standard eczema care. Bath-specific trials are lacking.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Possible oat allergy. The tub gets slippery.
- **Sources:** [Colloidal oatmeal formulations and atopic dermatitis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25607551/), [Colloidal oatmeal as adjunct in AD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22777219/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Children's eczema routine plus a skin symptom journal.

### Chamomile compress / bath (kamomill)
- **Use:** Irritated skin, nappy rash.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Central European tradition: cooled chamomile tea used as a compress or in the bath.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal.
- **Safety:** **Contact allergy, especially with daisy-family or ragweed allergy.**
- **Sources:** [Chamomile meta-analysis (PubMed 31006899)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006899/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library.

## Acne, fungal infections and lice

### Tea tree oil
- **Use:** Acne, athlete's foot, head lice, minor cuts.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Aboriginal Australian (Bundjalung) tradition of crushed *Melaleuca* leaves. Today, diluted essential oil (5% gels).
- **Evidence:** Promising for acne and athlete's foot (small studies). Insufficient evidence for other uses.
- **Safety:** **Never swallow it. Ingestion causes confusion, unsteadiness and coma, and is dangerous for children.** Skin irritation and allergy are common. Lavender and tea tree products have been linked to breast swelling in children (not proven).
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Tea tree oil](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/tea-tree-oil), [NCCIH: Lavender](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/lavender)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library with a "keep away from children" flag.

### Aloe vera for sunburn
- **Use:** Sunburn.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Mediterranean and Latin American tradition. Gel from the leaf.
- **Evidence:** Promising for burn healing (see minor-ailments-first-aid).
- **Safety:** Low risk topically.
- **Sources:** [2024 aloe burn meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38605441/)
