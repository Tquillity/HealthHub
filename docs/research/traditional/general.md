# General: traditional and folk remedies (cross-cutting safety)

Cross-cutting warnings that apply to every traditional remedy: "natural" is not automatically safe.

## Contamination and quality

### Ayurvedic and traditional medicines with heavy metals
- **Use:** Many uses: Ayurvedic rasa shastra preparations and imported traditional remedies.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Indian and South Asian traditional medicines, some made deliberately with metals or minerals (rasa shastra).
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (the risk is documented). About 20% of Ayurvedic products bought in Boston stores (2004) and online (2008) contained lead, mercury or arsenic. Half of the contaminated 2004 products were marketed for children.
- **Safety:** **Lead poisoning risk, especially for children and in pregnancy.** Avoid rasa shastra products. Buy only from regulated sources.
- **Sources:** [Saper 2004, JAMA: Heavy metal content of Ayurvedic products (CDC Stacks)](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/223634), [UW summary: Ayurvedic medicines and toxic metals](https://staff.washington.edu/chudler/ayur2.html)
- **HealthHub fit:** "Before you try a remedy" checklist in the remedy library: source, age, pregnancy, medications.

## Herb-drug interactions

### Herbal remedies that interact with medicines
- **Use:** Safety check for any household member on medication.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Key folk herbs with real interactions: St John's wort (many drugs, the pill), turmeric and curcumin (warfarin), garlic, ginger, feverfew and flaxseed (bleeding risk), liquorice (blood pressure, potassium), valerian (sedatives), apple cider vinegar (insulin, diuretics), fenugreek (blood sugar).
- **Evidence:** Proven (documented by NCCIH and medicines regulators).
- **Safety:** Tell the doctor or pharmacist about every herb or "kitchen remedy" taken regularly.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: St. John's wort](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort), [Medsafe NZ: Warfarin: eat, drink and be wary](https://medsafe.govt.nz/profs/PUArticles/December2022/Warfarin-eat-drink-and-be-wary.html), [NCCIH: Licorice root](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/licorice-root)
- **HealthHub fit:** Medication list in profile, cross-referenced against remedies the member logs. Core safety infrastructure for a remedy library.

## Age and pregnancy gates

### Remedies with hard age or pregnancy limits
- **Use:** A household safety layer.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A summary of limits found across this folder:
  - honey: never under 1
  - camphor rubs: none under 2
  - benzocaine teething gel: none under 2
  - fennel tea: not under 4
  - pelargonium: 12+
  - salt-water gargle: not for young children
  - star anise tea: never for infants
  - amber necklaces: never
  - steam bowls: avoid with children
  - alcohol in any remedy: never for children
  - fenugreek, liquorice, feverfew, black cohosh, castor oil: avoid in pregnancy
- **Evidence:** Proven (regulator and guideline positions cited in each entry).
- **Safety:** These are the most important warnings for the app.
- **Sources:** [NHS: Cough](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cough/), [Mayo Clinic: Vicks VapoRub](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/common-cold/expert-answers/nasal-decongestant/faq-20058569), [EMA: Foeniculum vulgare](https://www.ema.europa.eu/documents/herbal-report/assessment-report-foeniculum-vulgare-miller_en.pdf)
- **HealthHub fit:** Use household member age and pregnancy status (cycle feature) to show or hide remedies automatically.
