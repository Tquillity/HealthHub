# Cardio-metabolic: traditional and folk remedies

Folk habits and kitchen remedies for heart health, blood pressure and blood sugar, from Finnish sauna culture to cinnamon and vinegar.

## Heart and blood pressure

### Regular sauna bathing (bastu / sauna)
- **Use:** Heart health, blood pressure, longevity.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Finnish and Nordic tradition. About 80 °C for 15-20 minutes, several times a week, with cooling off in between.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Finnish cohort studies associate frequent sauna (4-7 times a week) with lower blood pressure, cardiovascular death, dementia and overall mortality. Most of the evidence is observational and from one research group.
- **Safety:** **Avoid with unstable angina, recent heart attack, severe aortic stenosis, alcohol and acute illness.** Get up slowly (low blood pressure, fainting). Caution in pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [Laukkanen 2018, Mayo Clinic Proceedings](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30077204/), [Laukkanen & Kunutsor 2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10989710/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Sauna-session habit tracker in routines, plotted against blood pressure in vitals.

### Liquorice root (lakritsrot) and salty liquorice
- **Use:** Folk use for coughs and stomach. In Nordic countries, mainly eaten as sweets.
- **What it is / how it's used:** European, Chinese (gan cao) and Middle Eastern herb.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful in regular large amounts.
- **Safety:** **Glycyrrhizin raises blood pressure and can cause irregular heartbeat. Even small amounts can be harmful with high blood pressure or heart or kidney disease. Unsafe in large amounts in pregnancy.**
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Licorice root](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/licorice-root)
- **HealthHub fit:** Warning in vitals when a member has high blood pressure.

### Garlic for "thin blood" and heart
- **Use:** Blood pressure, cholesterol.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Mediterranean, Eastern European and Asian tradition. Raw cloves or capsules.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal from the sources we reviewed (the cold-prevention evidence is weak). Note: the science lens may have more on blood pressure.
- **Safety:** Adds to the effect of blood thinners. Stop before surgery.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Garlic for the common cold](https://www.cochrane.org/CD006206/ARI_garlic-common-cold)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipe tags. Medication cross-check.

## Blood sugar and weight

### Apple cider vinegar before meals
- **Use:** Blood sugar, weight.
- **What it is / how it's used:** US (Vermont) folk medicine. 1-2 tablespoons diluted in water before meals.
- **Evidence:** Promising for a modest fasting-glucose drop in type 2 diabetes. Weak for weight.
- **Safety:** Enamel erosion, throat irritation. Interacts with insulin, diuretics and some blood-pressure drugs.
- **Sources:** [American Heart Association](https://www.heart.org/en/news/2024/09/11/what-can-apple-cider-vinegar-really-do-for-your-health), [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exploring-the-health-benefits-of-apple-cider-vinegar)
- **HealthHub fit:** Myth vs evidence card. Blood-sugar logging.

### Cinnamon (kanel)
- **Use:** Blood sugar control.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Ayurvedic, Chinese and Middle Eastern tradition. Cinnamon tea, or a teaspoon a day in food or capsules.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. NCCIH says research doesn't clearly show it helps diabetes.
- **Safety:** **Cassia cinnamon (common in Swedish kanel and kanelbullar) contains coumarin, which can harm the liver at high regular intake.** Ceylon cinnamon has much less.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Cinnamon](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/cinnamon)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipe note: prefer Ceylon cinnamon for daily use.
