# Pain and musculoskeletal: traditional and folk remedies

Folk remedies for joint pain, headaches and aches, from Danish rosehip to cupping.

## Joint pain

### Rosehip powder (nypon)
- **Use:** Osteoarthritis pain.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Nordic and European tradition: rosehip soup and tea. Danish rosehip powder (*Rosa canina*), about 5 g a day.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A meta-analysis of 3 RCTs (287 patients) found people taking rosehip were about twice as likely to respond (NNT 6). All three trials were funded by one manufacturer.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Possible stomach upset.
- **Sources:** [Christensen 2008 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18407528/)

### Turmeric
- **Use:** Joint pain, inflammation.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Ayurvedic tradition. Golden milk or supplements.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal at food doses (supplement trials are covered by the science lens).
- **Safety:** Rare liver injury. Warfarin interaction.
- **Sources:** [LiverTox: Turmeric](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548561/), [Medsafe NZ](https://medsafe.govt.nz/safety/EWS/2018/Turmeric.asp)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipe plus a medication warning.

## Headache and migraine

### Feverfew (mattram)
- **Use:** Migraine prevention, headaches, fever (the name means "fever few").
- **What it is / how it's used:** English and European cottage-garden herb. Fresh leaves chewed or capsules taken daily.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal to Promising. A review of 7 small studies gave inconsistent results, with some reduction in migraine frequency.
- **Safety:** Mouth ulcers when chewing leaves. Ragweed allergy. **Avoid in pregnancy. May slow blood clotting, so stop 2 weeks before surgery.**
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Feverfew](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/feverfew)

## Muscle aches

### Cupping (koppning, banki, hijama)
- **Use:** Back and neck pain, muscle aches.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Chinese, Middle Eastern, Russian and historic Swedish practice.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal (low-quality evidence for pain).
- **Safety:** Burns, scarring, infection. Bloodborne disease risk with wet cupping.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Cupping](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/cupping)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library (culture/history note).

### Sauna and heat
- **Use:** Muscle and joint aches.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Finnish sauna.
- **Evidence:** Promising (reviews report pain and musculoskeletal benefits, mostly from observational and small studies).
- **Safety:** See cardio-metabolic.
- **Sources:** [Laukkanen 2018 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30077204/)
