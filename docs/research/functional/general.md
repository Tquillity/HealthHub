# General: functional and integrative approaches

Cross-cutting systems of care (functional medicine, naturopathy, Ayurveda, TCM, homeopathy) plus general safety rules for supplements and "wellness" practices.

## Systems of care

### Functional medicine (funktionsmedicin) as a care model
- **Use:** Chronic, multi-symptom complaints (fatigue, gut, hormones, "root cause" of illness).
- **What it is / how it's used:** A practitioner-led model, popularised by the US Institute for Functional Medicine, that looks for "root causes" through long consultations, a detailed timeline, extensive lab testing and diet/lifestyle/supplement plans. At Cleveland Clinic every new patient also sees a dietitian and health coach.
- **Evidence:** Promising (for the lifestyle-heavy care package; observational only). A 2019 retrospective JAMA Network Open study of 7,252 patients found slightly larger improvements in self-rated physical health (PROMIS) at 6 months than matched primary-care patients (mean change about 1.6 points). Not randomised; groups differed at baseline; the effect may come from dietitian and coaching time rather than anything unique to functional medicine.
- **Safety:** The risks come from over-testing, costly supplement protocols and delayed conventional diagnosis. The American Academy of Family Physicians stopped giving course credit for functional medicine education over safety concerns.
- **Sources:** [Association of the Functional Medicine Model of Care With Patient-Reported HRQoL (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31651966/), [Cleveland Clinic press release](https://newsroom.clevelandclinic.org/2019/10/25/cleveland-clinic-study-finds-functional-medicine-model-is-associated-with-improvements-in-health-related-quality-of-life), [Science-Based Medicine critique](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/cleveland-clinic-publishes-functional-medicine/), [IFM summary of the study](https://www.ifm.org/news-insights/new-study-suggests-functional-medicine-improves-patient-outcomes/), [Functional medicine (Wikipedia)](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Functional_medicine)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library / education: keep the useful parts (symptom timeline, food-symptom journal, health-coach-style routines) and leave out the test-heavy parts. Idea: a "my health timeline" view built from journal and vitals.

### Swedish functional medicine clinics (e.g. Funmed) and the VoF critique
- **Use:** Context for Swedish users who are considering a private funktionsmedicin clinic.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Swedish private clinics offer "root cause" assessments built on broad blood, urine and stool panels, and some sell products. Training courses for Swedish healthcare staff exist (Funktionsmedicinska Vårdutbildningar).
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Vetenskap och Folkbildning named Funmed "Årets förvillare 2024" (Misleader of the Year). They criticise the over-reliance on testing: with so many analytes, many values fall outside reference ranges by chance, with no link to symptoms. In Folkvett, a paediatrician describes a 30,000 SEK child health check that included 44 food-antibody tests that no Swedish paediatric allergist would order. No published outcome data from the clinics appear in recognised journals.
- **Safety:** High cost, false positives leading to unnecessary diets and supplements, and potential to delay real diagnoses (especially in children).
- **Sources:** [VoF: Utförlig information om Funmed (2025, PDF)](https://vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Utforlig-information-om-Funmed.pdf), [VoF/Folkvett: Funktionell medicin (PDF)](https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/5-funktionll-medicin.pdf), [Diet Doctor: Intresset för funktionsmedicin ökar](https://www.dietdoctor.com/se/funktionsmedicin), [Humanisten nr 1 2025 (PDF)](https://humanisterna.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Humanisten-nr-1-2025.pdf)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results: a "before you buy a test panel" checklist (What decision will this change? Is the test validated? Who interprets it?).

### Naturopathy
- **Use:** General "natural" care, often combining diet, herbs, homeopathy and hydrotherapy.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A whole system run by naturopaths. Licensing varies by country and US state. Use is growing (US adults: 0.2% in 2002, 1.3% in 2022).
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. NCCIH reports usage figures but no condition where naturopathy as a system has been shown to work. Individual parts (some herbs, diet changes) have their own evidence, covered in other entries.
- **Safety:** Licences "do not guarantee safe, effective treatment". Tell your doctor about everything you use.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Naturopathy](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/naturopathy)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: tag each remedy with where it comes from (naturopathy, TCM, Ayurveda, folk) and show its evidence level separately.

### Homeopathy
- **Use:** Claimed for nearly everything (colds, allergies, teething, anxiety).
- **What it is / how it's used:** Extremely diluted substances ("like cures like"), often diluted until no molecule of the starting substance remains.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. Australia's NHMRC (2015) reviewed 225 controlled studies and found no health condition with reliable evidence of benefit beyond placebo. Its core principles conflict with basic chemistry and physics. (Homeopathy groups dispute the review's methods.)
- **Safety:** Some products labelled "homeopathic" are not dilute: the FDA has warned about teething tablets with too much belladonna. Some liquids contain alcohol. The main harm is replacing effective care or vaccination.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Homeopathy](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/homeopathy), [The Conversation: Health experts find no evidence homeopathy works, again](https://theconversation.com/health-experts-find-no-evidence-homeopathy-works-again-38651), [HRI critique of NHMRC (Thieme)](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0038-1633302)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library warning card: "No better than placebo; never use instead of vaccines or for serious illness."

### Ayurveda
- **Use:** A whole-system traditional Indian medicine: dosha-based diet, herbs, oil massage, yoga.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Individualised herbal formulas and lifestyle rules. Rasa shastra products deliberately combine herbs with metals or minerals.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Few good trials. One 440-person knee osteoarthritis trial found Ayurvedic formulas comparable to glucosamine and celecoxib. Evidence for diabetes and other conditions is weak.
- **Safety:** Likely harmful for some products. A 2008 JAMA study found that 20.7% of Ayurvedic medicines bought online contained lead, mercury and/or arsenic (40.6% of rasa shastra products). A survey found raised blood lead in 40% of users. Avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding, and avoid imported products without heavy-metal testing.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Ayurvedic Medicine In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ayurvedic-medicine-in-depth), [Saper et al., JAMA 2008](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/1108395), [BU: Ayurvedic Medicines Merit a Closer Look](https://www.bu.edu/articles/2008/ayurvedic-medicines-merit-a-closer-look/?nb=1)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: keep the daily-routine ideas (dinacharya, gentle movement, warm cooked meals) as routines; show a heavy-metal warning on any herbal product entry.

### Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) herbal products
- **Use:** Whole-system care: herbal formulas, acupuncture, tai chi/qigong, cupping.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Multi-herb formulas prescribed by pattern diagnosis (tongue, pulse).
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal for herbal formulas ("many studies of poor quality, no firm conclusions"). Acupuncture and tai chi have their own, better evidence (see pain and sleep files).
- **Safety:** Likely harmful when products are unregulated. Documented contamination includes undeclared drugs (e.g. warfarin), heavy metals (arsenic, lead, cadmium), pesticides and substituted herbs that can cause organ damage.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Traditional Chinese Medicine: What You Need To Know](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/traditional-chinese-medicine-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: list tai chi and qigong as routines; flag imported herbal formulas as "check source and interactions".

## General safety rules for supplements and herbs

### Herbal and dietary supplement liver injury
- **Use:** A safety layer for any supplement protocol (functional medicine plans often stack 5-15 products).
- **What it is / how it's used:** In the US DILIN liver-injury registry, supplements rose from about 7% of drug-induced liver injury cases (2004-05) to 20% (2013-14). Products named most often include turmeric/curcumin (especially high-bioavailability forms), green tea extract, garcinia, kratom, ashwagandha, black cohosh and kava.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (as a risk signal: this is real, documented harm).
- **Safety:** Stop and seek care if you get fatigue, dark urine, abdominal pain or yellow skin/eyes. Green tea as a drink is fine; concentrated extracts are not the same thing.
- **Sources:** [Navarro et al. review (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502701), [Science-Based Medicine: supplements linked to liver damage](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/widespread-use-of-dietary-supplements-linked-to-liver-damage/), [NCCIH: Turmeric](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric), [NCCIH: Ashwagandha](https://nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker: a per-household "supplement cabinet" that flags liver-risk products and shows the symptoms to watch for.

### Swedish rules: naturläkemedel, växtbaserade läkemedel and interactions
- **Use:** Help Swedish households choose and combine herbal products safely.
- **What it is / how it's used:** In Sweden, herbal medicines and "naturläkemedel" must be approved by Läkemedelsverket and carry that label. Plain food supplements (kosttillskott) do not need approval. 1177 warns that herbal remedies can weaken blood thinners and contraceptive pills, and that St John's wort keeps affecting other drugs for about 2 weeks after you stop it.
- **Evidence:** Proven (as regulatory and safety guidance from a national health authority).
- **Safety:** Generally not recommended in pregnancy or breastfeeding. Report suspected side effects to Läkemedelsverket.
- **Sources:** [1177: Växtbaserade läkemedel och naturläkemedel](https://www.1177.se/undersokning-behandling/fler-behandlingar/alternativa-behandlingar/vaxtbaserade-lakemedel-och-naturlakemedel/), [Livsmedelsverket: Risker med växtbaserade kosttillskott och örtteer under graviditet och amning (PDF)](https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/globalassets/publikationsdatabas/rapporter/2021/l-2021-nr-25-risker-med-vaxtbaserade-kosttillskott-och-ortteer-under-graviditet-och-amning.pdf)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker: a "Godkänt naturläkemedel / kosttillskott" field per product plus an interaction reminder whenever a household member also logs medicines.

### Supplement label accuracy
- **Use:** Context for any supplement dose advice.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A 2023 study found 22 of 25 melatonin gummies mislabelled, with actual content ranging from 74% to 347% of the stated dose. US supplements are not approved before sale.
- **Evidence:** Proven (documented problem).
- **Safety:** Prefer products with third-party testing or Swedish approval as a medicine. Store out of children's reach.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Melatonin: What You Need To Know](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker: optional "third-party tested?" field and a safe-storage reminder for households with children.

## Popular wellness practices

### Detox diets and juice cleanses
- **Use:** "Remove toxins", lose weight, reset.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Multi-day juice fasts, detox teas and supplement kits.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. Reviews find "no compelling research" that they remove toxins. Early weight loss comes from eating fewer calories and is usually regained.
- **Safety:** Unpasteurised juice can cause serious infection (children, older adults, immunocompromised). High-oxalate juices can harm people prone to kidney stones. Expect electrolyte problems with severe restriction. The FDA and FTC have acted against detox products with hidden ingredients.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Detoxes and Cleanses](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library warning card. Offer a "fibre and vegetables week" recipe plan as the evidence-based alternative.

### Grounding / earthing
- **Use:** Claimed to improve sleep, reduce pain, inflammation and stress.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Walking barefoot on soil or grass, or sleeping on conductive mats or sheets connected to an outlet's earth pin.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Only small pilot studies (about 50 people at most), many by product-linked authors. A 2023 review notes indoor-grounding studies don't control for confounders. Australia's Choice gave grounding socks a "Shonky" award.
- **Safety:** Barefoot time outdoors is low risk. Products are a waste of money; electrical mats wired incorrectly could be unsafe.
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic: Earthing](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/earthing), [Science-Based Medicine: Earthing update](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/earthing-update/), [Choice Shonky Awards 2024: grounding socks](https://www.choice.com.au/shonky-awards/hall-of-shame/shonkys-2024/groundingwell-grounding-socks), [Chevalier et al. 2012 proponent review (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22291721/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine: "10 minutes outside, barefoot if you like" (the time outdoors and daylight is the likely benefit). Skip product links.

### Cold-water immersion / cold showers
- **Use:** Wellbeing, stress resilience, sleep, immunity ("Wim Hof" style).
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cold showers, winter swimming (vinterbad) or ice baths, usually 30 seconds to a few minutes.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2025 PLOS One meta-analysis of 11 studies (3,177 people) found better quality of life and sleep (sleep data were from men only), no change in mood, and a short-term rise in inflammatory markers. A 29% drop in sick days among people taking cold showers came from a single study.
- **Safety:** Cold shock can trigger heart rhythm problems or drowning. Avoid with heart disease, uncontrolled blood pressure or Raynaud's, and in pregnancy without medical advice. Never swim alone in cold water.
- **Sources:** [Cain et al., PLOS One 2025 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879231/), [ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/01/250131110704.htm)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine + tracker: "cold shower streak" with a mood/sleep journal prompt so users can test it on themselves (n-of-1).
