# Respiratory & immune: functional and integrative approaches

Herbal and home remedies, "immune boosting" practices and homeopathic products for colds, coughs and flu.

## Herbal remedies

### Echinacea
- **Use:** Prevent or shorten colds.
- **What it is / how it's used:** E. purpurea extract or tablets at the first sign of a cold, or as a prevention course.
- **Evidence:** Promising. It may modestly lower the chance of catching a cold; it is unclear whether it shortens one.
- **Safety:** Likely safe short-term for adults. Allergic reactions can be severe (daisy-family allergy). Ask a clinician before giving it to children or using it in pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Echinacea](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/echinacea)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library + household: a "cold season kit" list with evidence badges.

### Elderberry (fläder)
- **Use:** Cold and flu symptoms.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cooked elderberry syrup or lozenges.
- **Evidence:** Promising (preliminary). Only a few small studies.
- **Safety:** Raw or unripe berries, leaves and stems contain cyanide-producing compounds that cause vomiting and diarrhoea; cooking removes the toxin. Safety in pregnancy is unknown. Relevant to Swedish households that make their own fläder products: elderflower cordial is different from berry syrup.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: European Elder](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/european-elder)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: a home elderberry syrup recipe with a "must be cooked" safety step.

### Honey for cough
- **Use:** Acute cough in children (over 1 year) and adults.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 1-2 teaspoons before bed or in warm drinks.
- **Evidence:** Proven (low-to-moderate certainty). A Cochrane review found that honey probably relieves cough better than placebo or no treatment, about as well as dextromethorphan, for up to 3 days.
- **Safety:** Never give to infants under 12 months (infant botulism risk).
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Honey for acute cough in children](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007094/ARI_honey-for-acute-cough-in-children)

## "Immune boosting" practices

### Cold showers / cold exposure for immunity
- **Use:** Fewer colds, "stronger immune system".
- **What it is / how it's used:** Ending a shower with 30-90 seconds of cold water.
- **Evidence:** Promising (weak). A 2025 meta-analysis found no measurable change in immune markers. One included study reported 29% fewer sick days with cold showers.
- **Safety:** Cold shock risk with heart disease.
- **Sources:** [Cain et al., PLOS One 2025 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879231/), [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1072266)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine + household sick-day log, so a family can see their own pattern.

### Probiotics for immunity
- **Use:** Fewer infections.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Daily probiotic capsules or fermented foods.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal for general "immune boosting". Better evidence exists only for preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhoea (see nutrition file).
- **Safety:** Avoid in immunocompromised people and premature infants.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Probiotics](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/probiotics-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: fermented foods (yoghurt, kefir, sauerkraut) rather than supplements.

## Homeopathic products

### Homeopathic cold and flu remedies
- **Use:** Colds, flu, allergies.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Highly diluted pills or drops.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. NHMRC: no reliable evidence for any condition.
- **Safety:** Delays effective care. Must not replace vaccination.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Homeopathy](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/homeopathy), [The Conversation: No evidence homeopathy is effective](https://theconversation.com/no-evidence-homeopathy-is-effective-nhmrc-review-25368)
