# Cardio-metabolic: functional and integrative approaches

"Blood-sugar balancing", fasting, anti-inflammatory eating and metabolic supplements for blood sugar, weight, blood pressure and heart health.

## Blood-sugar balancing

### Continuous glucose monitors (CGM) for people without diabetes
- **Use:** "Flatten glucose spikes" to improve energy, weight and long-term health.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Wear a 14-day sensor, see how meals affect glucose and adjust food order, portions or after-meal walks.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal (for health benefit). Stanford data show that many healthy people have occasional high spikes (almost everyone spiked after cornflakes), but no trial shows that reacting to spikes improves outcomes in people without diabetes. Larger glucose responses are associated with stiffer arteries, but that is not proof they cause harm.
- **Safety:** Cost (about 500-1,000 SEK per sensor). Can encourage obsessive eating or needless carbohydrate fear. Useful if it uncovers undiagnosed prediabetes, which should then be confirmed with HbA1c.
- **Sources:** [Stanford Medicine: Diabetic-level glucose spikes seen in healthy people](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2018/07/diabetic-level-glucose-spikes-seen-in-healthy-people.html), [NBC News: Is there a benefit for healthy people to track blood sugar?](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/heart-health/glucose-monitoring-blood-sugar-tracking-continuous-non-diabetic-know-rcna189881), [Glucose profiles in adults without diabetes (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10973852)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals: optional CGM/glucose import with meal tagging; show "normal post-meal rise" bands rather than alarming on every spike.

### "Glucose hacks": vinegar before meals
- **Use:** Lower after-meal glucose; popular "Glucose Goddess"-style tip.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 1-2 tablespoons of apple cider vinegar in a glass of water before carbohydrate-heavy meals.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2025 meta-analysis of 7 trials in type 2 diabetes found lower fasting glucose and HbA1c, with more effect above 10 ml/day. Trials are small and varied.
- **Safety:** Always dilute. Erodes tooth enamel and can irritate the throat. Can lower potassium at high intake. Monitor glucose if you take diabetes medicines.
- **Sources:** [ACV and glycaemic control in T2D: GRADE-assessed meta-analysis (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11821484/), [2021 meta-analysis in BMC Complement Med Ther (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34187442/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: vinegar-dressed salad starters; a "veg first, carbs last" meal-order tip in the meal planner.

### Berberine ("nature's Ozempic")
- **Use:** Blood sugar, weight, cholesterol.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Plant alkaloid capsules, typically 500 mg 2-3 times daily with meals.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Meta-analyses (mostly Chinese trials, varied in quality) show lower fasting glucose (about 0.6 mmol/L), lower after-meal glucose and lower HbA1c in type 2 diabetes. The weight-loss claims are oversold.
- **Safety:** Stomach upset and constipation. Interactions with diabetes drugs and drugs broken down by the liver are not well characterised. Avoid in pregnancy and in infants.
- **Sources:** [Glucose-lowering effect of berberine on T2D: meta-analysis (Frontiers 2022)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2022.1015045/full), [2024 meta-analysis (Frontiers)](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphar.2024.1455534/full)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker with a "talk to your doctor if on diabetes medicine" flag; not something to recommend proactively.

## Fasting and eating windows

### Intermittent fasting / time-restricted eating (16:8, early eating)
- **Use:** Weight loss, insulin sensitivity, "metabolic health", longevity.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Eating within a 6-10 hour window daily, or 5:2 fasting.
- **Evidence:** Proven (works about as well as ordinary calorie restriction, not better). The NEJM 2022 12-month RCT found no extra weight loss or metabolic benefit from 8-hour eating plus calorie restriction vs calorie restriction alone. A 2023 trial agreed. Longevity claims come from animal data.
- **Safety:** Not for people with diabetes on insulin or sulfonylureas without supervision, pregnancy, eating disorders, or children.
- **Sources:** [Liu et al., NEJM 2022 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35443107/), [Lin et al. 2023, Annals (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37364268/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner + timer: optional eating-window tracker framed as "a way to eat less if it suits you", with hunger/energy journaling.

## Anti-inflammatory eating

### Anti-inflammatory diet (low Dietary Inflammatory Index)
- **Use:** Heart health, "chronic inflammation".
- **What it is / how it's used:** More vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, oily fish, olive oil, nuts, spices; less processed meat, refined carbs and fried foods (much like Mediterranean/Nordic diets).
- **Evidence:** Promising. Meta-analyses of cohort studies link more pro-inflammatory diets to higher CVD risk (about 8% per DII point) and higher all-cause mortality. Observational data with high heterogeneity; overlaps with well-proven Mediterranean diet evidence.
- **Safety:** Low risk.
- **Sources:** [DII and cardiovascular risk: updated meta-analysis 2025 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12675463/), [DII and CVD meta-analysis 2018 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5852776/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: an "anti-inflammatory" tag and a weekly plate score (plants, fish, legumes vs processed foods) on the dashboard.

### Detox diets for weight loss
- **Use:** Quick weight loss.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Juice cleanses, detox teas.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. The early weight loss is regained; there are no long-term studies.
- **Safety:** Electrolyte problems; some detox teas contain laxatives or hidden ingredients.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Detoxes and Cleanses](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library warning card.

## Supplements

### Omega-3 for triglycerides and heart
- **Use:** High triglycerides, heart protection.
- **What it is / how it's used:** EPA+DHA 1-4 g/day (prescription forms differ from supplements).
- **Evidence:** Proven for lowering triglycerides (about 15%). Promising for fewer heart attacks (VITAL secondary outcome; meta-analyses show small reductions in coronary events).
- **Safety:** Mild GI effects. Check with your clinician if you take anticoagulants.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Omega-3 Supplements](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/omega3-supplements-what-you-need-to-know), [Healio: VITAL omega-3 results](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20181207/vital-vitamin-d-omega3-fail-to-reduce-major-cv-events-cancer)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results: if triglycerides are logged high, suggest oily-fish meals and a discussion with a clinician.

### Vitamin D for heart-disease prevention
- **Use:** Heart protection.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Daily D3.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (no effect). VITAL: no reduction in major cardiovascular events (HR 0.97).
- **Safety:** Safe at 2,000 IU/day; toxicity at very high doses.
- **Sources:** [VITAL principal results (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31733345/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker evidence note.

## Mind-body for blood pressure and metabolism

### Tai chi for blood pressure and type 2 diabetes
- **Use:** Blood pressure, HbA1c, heart-disease rehab.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Regular tai chi practice, several times a week.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Reviews show benefits for blood pressure, aerobic capacity and HbA1c, but not clearly better than other aerobic exercise. Many studies are small.
- **Safety:** Low risk.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Tai Chi: What You Need To Know](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/tai-chi-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: tai chi counted as activity minutes; BP trend in vitals.

### Mindfulness (MBSR) and slow breathing for blood pressure
- **Use:** High blood pressure.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 8-week MBSR course, or daily 5-10 minutes of slow breathing at about 6 breaths/min.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2020 review of 14 studies linked MBSR to significant BP reductions in people with hypertension, though high-quality studies are few. Slow breathing lowers BP and raises HRV in the short term.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Do not stop BP medicines.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Meditation and Mindfulness](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety), [Cornell: The benefits of deep breathing](https://evidencebasedliving.human.cornell.edu/blog/the-benefits-of-deep-breathing-and-why-it-works)
- **HealthHub fit:** Vitals + timer: "breathe, then measure" BP routine, so users can see their own before/after readings.
