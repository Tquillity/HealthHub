# Pain & musculoskeletal: functional and integrative approaches

Acupuncture, manual therapies (chiropractic, osteopathy), mind-body movement and herbal anti-inflammatories for back pain, arthritis, migraine and joint pain.

## Acupuncture

### Acupuncture for migraine prevention
- **Use:** Reduce how often episodic migraines happen.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A course of at least 6 sessions with a trained practitioner.
- **Evidence:** Proven (moderate certainty). The 2016 Cochrane review (22 trials, 4,985 people) found headache frequency at least halved in 41/100 with acupuncture vs 17/100 with usual care. It may be about as effective as preventive drugs, with fewer side effects. The advantage over sham needling is small, so needle placement seems to matter less than practitioners assume.
- **Safety:** Minor bruising and pain. Rare serious harms with poor technique or non-sterile needles.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Acupuncture for the prevention of episodic migraine](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001218), [Linde et al. 2016 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27351677/), [NCCIH: Acupuncture: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/acupuncture-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Headache diary: log migraine days before and after a course of treatment to see personal effect.

### Acupuncture for chronic low back pain
- **Use:** Chronic non-specific low back pain.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Weekly sessions for 6-12 weeks.
- **Evidence:** Promising. The 2020 Cochrane review found it better than no treatment for pain and function right after treatment, but little different from sham. NICE NG59 says do not offer acupuncture for low back pain.
- **Safety:** Adverse events are similar to sham (bruising, insertion pain).
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Acupuncture for chronic non-specific low back pain](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013814), [NICE NG59 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendation)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library entry: "may help some people short-term; exercise first".

## Manual therapies

### Spinal manipulation (chiropractic, osteopathy, naprapathy) for low back pain
- **Use:** Acute and chronic low back pain.
- **What it is / how it's used:** High-velocity thrusts, mobilisation and massage by a chiropractor, osteopath, naprapath or physiotherapist. Naprapaths and chiropractors are licensed in Sweden.
- **Evidence:** Proven (as part of a package). NICE NG59 says consider manual therapy only alongside exercise, with or without psychological therapy. NCCIH: modest improvement in acute low back pain; mixed results for chronic pain.
- **Safety:** Temporary soreness or stiffness (usually gone within 24 hours) is common.
- **Sources:** [NICE NG59 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendation), [NCCIH: Spinal Manipulation](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/spinal-manipulation-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: back-pain exercise program as the core, with "manual therapy can be added" as an optional step.

### Neck (cervical) manipulation
- **Use:** Neck pain, cervicogenic headache.
- **What it is / how it's used:** High-velocity neck thrusts.
- **Evidence:** Promising for acute neck pain and cervicogenic headache.
- **Safety:** Very rare but serious: tears in neck arteries (vertebral artery dissection) and stroke. One estimate is about 1.3 cases per 100,000 people under 45 manipulated within the past week. Whether manipulation actually causes these tears is debated. Gentler mobilisation is an alternative.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Spinal Manipulation](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/spinal-manipulation-what-you-need-to-know), [Review: dangers of neck manipulation (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6016850/), [ACA: Vertebral artery dissection evidence review](https://www.acatoday.org/news-publications/vertebral-artery-dissection-an-evidence-review/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: red-flag card (sudden severe headache, dizziness, vision or speech changes after neck treatment → emergency care).

### Chiropractic or osteopathy for non-musculoskeletal conditions
- **Use:** Claimed for infant colic, asthma, ear infections, digestion, "immunity".
- **What it is / how it's used:** Spinal "adjustments" for problems not related to the spine.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. Research is limited, and none of the high-quality studies show a clear benefit.
- **Safety:** Delays effective care. Infants and children are especially vulnerable.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Spinal Manipulation](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/spinal-manipulation-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household/children: warning in the remedy library.

## Mind-body movement

### Yoga for chronic low back pain
- **Use:** Chronic non-specific back pain.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Weekly classes plus home practice for 8-12 weeks.
- **Evidence:** Proven (small effect). The 2022 Cochrane review (21 trials) found small improvements in pain and function vs no exercise, about the same as other back exercise.
- **Safety:** Temporary back pain is the most common harm; no serious harms.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Yoga for chronic non-specific low back pain](https://www.cochrane.org/CD010671/BACK_yoga-chronic-non-specific-low-back-pain), [Wieland et al. 2022 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36398843/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: a "back care" yoga routine with a pain score before and after.

### Tai chi for knee osteoarthritis
- **Use:** Knee and hip osteoarthritis pain and function.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Classes twice weekly for 12 weeks, then home practice.
- **Evidence:** Proven. A 204-person RCT found tai chi as effective as standard physiotherapy at 52 weeks. The American College of Rheumatology strongly recommends it for knee and hip OA.
- **Safety:** Low risk.
- **Sources:** [Wang et al. 2016, tai chi vs physical therapy (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27183035/), [NCCIH research spotlight](https://nccih.nih.gov/research/results/spotlight/tai-chi-knee-osteoarthritis_2016), [NCCIH: Tai Chi](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/tai-chi-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: a guided tai chi routine for older household members with joint pain.

### Tai chi for fibromyalgia
- **Use:** Fibromyalgia pain, sleep, function.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Twice-weekly classes for 12 weeks.
- **Evidence:** Promising. An NEJM 2010 RCT and smaller studies are positive.
- **Safety:** Low risk.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Tai Chi](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/tai-chi-what-you-need-to-know), [Painscience: tai chi for pain](https://www.painscience.com/articles/tai-chi-for-pain.php)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines tag "fibromyalgia-friendly".

### Mindfulness for chronic pain
- **Use:** Chronic back pain, pain-related distress.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 8-week MBSR course.
- **Evidence:** Promising (short-term back pain benefit; not for fibromyalgia pain; low-quality chronic pain studies).
- **Safety:** Low risk.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Meditation and Mindfulness](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal prompts on pain acceptance; timer-based body scan.

## Herbal and nutritional anti-inflammatories

### Turmeric / curcumin
- **Use:** Knee osteoarthritis, "inflammation".
- **What it is / how it's used:** Curcumin extract capsules (often with piperine or "bioavailable" formulas), or turmeric in cooking.
- **Evidence:** Promising for knee osteoarthritis (initial positive evidence; better studies needed). Not enough evidence for most other uses.
- **Safety:** Liver damage reported, especially with highly bioavailable products. Stomach upset. Avoid supplements in pregnancy. Cooking amounts are fine.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Turmeric](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric), [Navarro et al. review of supplement liver injury (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502701)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: golden milk and curries as the food-first option; supplement tracker liver flag.

### Ayurvedic formulations for knee osteoarthritis
- **Use:** Knee OA pain.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Standardised multi-herb formulas.
- **Evidence:** Promising. One 440-person trial found results similar to glucosamine and celecoxib.
- **Safety:** Heavy-metal contamination risk in unregulated products.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Ayurvedic Medicine In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ayurvedic-medicine-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library entry with a product-quality warning.

### Omega-3 for rheumatoid arthritis
- **Use:** Joint pain and swelling in RA.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Fish oil supplements alongside usual treatment.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2022 review of 30 studies found improved pain, swelling and tenderness.
- **Safety:** Mild GI effects.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Omega-3 Supplements](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/omega3-supplements-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Symptom tracker for joint pain and supplement log.

## Folk and wellness claims

### Castor oil packs for joint or period pain
- **Use:** Joint pain, cramps.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Warm oil-soaked cloth on the painful area.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Any benefit probably comes from the warmth.
- **Safety:** Low risk.
- **Sources:** [Lead Stories fact check on castor oil compresses](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2023/04/fact-check-castor-oil-compress-not-proven-to-treat-diarrhea-constipation.html)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: list under "heat therapy" honestly.

### Grounding for pain and inflammation
- **Use:** Chronic pain, inflammation.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Conductive mats or patches.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Small, poorly controlled pilot studies.
- **Safety:** Low risk; cost.
- **Sources:** [Science-Based Medicine: Earthing update](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/earthing-update/), [Cleveland Clinic: Earthing](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/earthing)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: "unproven" label.
