# Mental health & stress: functional and integrative approaches

Adaptogens, "adrenal" protocols, diet-for-mood and mind-body practices for stress, anxiety, low mood and burnout.

## Diagnoses and claims to handle carefully

### "Adrenal fatigue" / "HPA axis dysfunction"
- **Use:** Explains burnout, fatigue, brain fog and "wired but tired" feelings in functional medicine.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Claims that chronic stress "exhausts" the adrenal glands. Diagnosed with saliva cortisol curves and treated with adaptogens, adrenal glandulars, vitamin C and B vitamins.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. A 2016 systematic review of 58 studies found no consistent evidence; no endocrine society recognises it. (Real adrenal insufficiency is rare and is diagnosed with validated tests.)
- **Safety:** Glandular products can contain active hormones. The bigger risk is missing depression, sleep apnoea, thyroid disease, anaemia or real burnout (utmattningssyndrom) that needs care.
- **Sources:** [Adrenal fatigue does not exist: a systematic review (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4997656/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: reframe as "stress and energy" with a mood/energy/sleep tracker and a "when to see a doctor" checklist.

## Adaptogens and herbs

### Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera)
- **Use:** Stress, anxiety, sleep.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Root extract, typically 300-600 mg/day.
- **Evidence:** Promising. NIH summaries say extracts may reduce stress and anxiety, with the best results at ≥600 mg/day for ≥8 weeks. Trials are small and short.
- **Safety:** Probably safe up to 3 months; long-term safety is unknown. Rare liver injury, including fatal cases or transplant in people with liver disease (avoid with cirrhosis). May affect thyroid function and interact with thyroid medicines. Avoid in pregnancy. Restricted in Denmark.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Ashwagandha](https://nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha), [LiverTox: Ashwagandha](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548536/), [Which?: Ashwagandha, stress buster or health risk?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/ashwagandha-stress-buster-or-health-risk-aw2Ik4l90Kpv)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker: 12-week course limit with liver and thyroid flags; pair with a weekly stress score.

### Rhodiola rosea (rosenrot)
- **Use:** Stress-related fatigue, burnout, mental performance. Nordic tradition; sold in Sweden as an approved traditional herbal medicine.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Standardised root extract (often 200-600 mg/day) for a few weeks.
- **Evidence:** Promising (weak). A 2012 systematic review found contradictory trials, all with high risk of bias. A more optimistic 2022 narrative review exists. NCCIH: not enough reliable evidence for any use.
- **Safety:** Possibly safe up to 12 weeks. Dizziness, insomnia, dry mouth. Reported interaction with losartan. Avoid in pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [Ishaque et al. 2012 systematic review (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3541197/), [NCCIH: Rhodiola](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/rhodiola), [2022 review of Rhodiola for stress (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9228580/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: a Nordic herb card; n-of-1 energy tracking.

### St John's wort (johannesört)
- **Use:** Mild to moderate low mood.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Standardised extract (approved herbal medicine in Sweden), taken for 4-12 weeks.
- **Evidence:** Proven (for mild-moderate depression in trials). A Cochrane review of 29 trials found it better than placebo and similar to standard antidepressants, though results varied by country.
- **Safety:** Likely harmful when combined with other medicines. It is a strong enzyme inducer (CYP3A4) and makes contraceptive pills, blood thinners, HIV drugs and many others less effective, with effects lasting about 2 weeks after stopping. Do not combine with SSRIs.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: St John's wort for depression](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000448), [1177: Växtbaserade läkemedel och naturläkemedel](https://www.1177.se/undersokning-behandling/fler-behandlingar/alternativa-behandlingar/vaxtbaserade-lakemedel-och-naturlakemedel/), [RCT: St John's wort and low-dose oral contraceptives (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14616430/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Interaction checker: hard warning if St John's wort and the contraceptive pill (or other medicines) are logged by the same household member.

## Mind-body practices

### Mindfulness meditation / MBSR
- **Use:** Anxiety, low mood, stress.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 8-week MBSR course (weekly group sessions + 20-45 minutes daily home practice) or app-based daily practice.
- **Evidence:** Proven (modest effect). A JAMA Internal Medicine meta-analysis of 47 RCTs found moderate evidence of improved anxiety (effect size 0.38) and depression (0.30) at 8 weeks, but not better than other active treatments. Later analyses find it similar to CBT, with short-term effects.
- **Safety:** Low risk. About 8% report negative effects (anxiety, low mood). Be careful with trauma or psychosis.
- **Sources:** [Goyal et al. 2014, meditation programs for stress (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24395196/), [NCCIH: Meditation and Mindfulness](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines + timer: an 8-week mindfulness program built on the existing timer, with a weekly stress check-in in the journal.

### Slow-paced breathing / breathwork
- **Use:** Acute stress, anxiety, winding down.
- **What it is / how it's used:** About 6 breaths per minute (e.g. 4-5 s in, 5-6 s out), box breathing, or extended exhale for 5-10 minutes.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2023 Scientific Reports meta-analysis of RCTs found small-to-moderate reductions in stress, anxiety (g = -0.32) and depressive symptoms (g = -0.40). Slow-paced breathing taught one-to-one looked best. Separate reviews show immediate effects on blood pressure and heart-rate variability.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Fast "hyperventilation" styles (e.g. Wim Hof) can cause fainting; never practise them in water or while driving.
- **Sources:** [Fincham et al. 2023, breathwork meta-analysis (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828383/), [News-Medical summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20230113/Review-and-meta-analysis-suggests-breathwork-may-be-effective-for-improving-stress-and-mental-health.aspx)
- **HealthHub fit:** Timer: a "breathing pacer" mode (animated 6 breaths/min) that can be added to any routine; log pre/post stress rating.

### Yoga for anxiety
- **Use:** Anxiety symptoms, stress.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Classes or home practice 2-3 times a week.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2019 review of 38 studies found a substantial effect on anxiety symptoms; less clear for diagnosed anxiety disorders (CBT did better in one trial).
- **Safety:** Generally safe. Sprains and strains are the most common injuries.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Yoga: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: short yoga sessions tagged "stress".

## Food and mood

### Mediterranean-style diet for depression (SMILES)
- **Use:** Adjunct for low mood and depression.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A modified Mediterranean diet (vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, olive oil, nuts, less ultra-processed food) with dietitian support.
- **Evidence:** Promising. The SMILES RCT (67 people) found 32% remission vs 8% with social support at 12 weeks. Small and unblinded, but backed by later trials and lots of observational data.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Use it alongside treatment, not instead of it.
- **Sources:** [Jacka et al. 2017, SMILES trial (BMC Medicine)](https://bmcmedicine.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12916-017-0791-y)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner: a "food & mood" plan with Mediterranean recipes and a daily mood rating in the journal.

### Omega-3 for depression
- **Use:** Low mood.
- **What it is / how it's used:** EPA-rich fish oil, often 1-2 g/day.
- **Evidence:** Promising (weak). A 2021 review of 35 studies found any effect may be too small to be meaningful (low-quality evidence). High DHA intake in pregnancy may reduce symptoms during pregnancy.
- **Safety:** Mild GI effects. Check with your clinician if you take blood thinners.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Omega-3 Supplements](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/omega3-supplements-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner: oily-fish recipes rather than promising a mood effect.
