# Nutrition & digestion: functional and integrative approaches

Elimination diets, gut "repair" protocols, probiotics and topical folk remedies for IBS, bloating and constipation.

## Elimination diets

### Low-FODMAP diet (elimination, then reintroduction)
- **Use:** IBS, bloating, wind, irregular bowels.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 2-6 weeks of strict reduction of fermentable carbohydrates (some wheat, onion, garlic, legumes, lactose, certain fruits). Foods are then reintroduced group by group to find personal triggers. Ideally guided by a dietitian.
- **Evidence:** Proven. Multiple meta-analyses and umbrella reviews show moderate improvements in IBS symptoms and quality of life. A 2022 network meta-analysis ranked it first among diets. Certainty is moderate to low because diet trials cannot be blinded.
- **Safety:** The strict phase can reduce beneficial gut bacteria and fibre, so do not stay in it long-term. Use caution with a history of eating disorders.
- **Sources:** [Van Lanen et al. 2021 meta-analysis (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8354978/), [Network meta-analysis 2022 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34376515/), [Umbrella review of meta-analyses (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12807944/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner + journal: a low-FODMAP recipe filter, a phased plan (eliminate → reintroduce → personalise) and a daily symptom score linked to meals.

### Broad functional "elimination diet" (gluten, dairy, soy, eggs, sugar, etc.)
- **Use:** Functional medicine "5R/remove" phase for fatigue, gut, skin or joint complaints.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Remove several common food groups for 3-6 weeks, then reintroduce. The foods to remove are often picked from IgG test results.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal (beyond the specific evidence for low-FODMAP in IBS). Choosing foods from IgG tests is disproven: positive IgG results are normal.
- **Safety:** Nutrient gaps (calcium, iodine, B12), especially in children. Can mask coeliac disease if gluten is removed before testing. Food anxiety.
- **Sources:** [CSACI position statement on IgG testing](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3443017/), [VoF/Folkvett: Funktionell medicin (PDF)](https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/5-funktionll-medicin.pdf)
- **HealthHub fit:** Journal: a structured "one food at a time" experiment tool with a reminder to get coeliac testing before cutting gluten.

## Gut microbiome protocols

### Probiotics for IBS
- **Use:** IBS pain, bloating, irregularity.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Daily capsules or fermented products, usually tried for 4-8 weeks.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2018 review of 53 trials suggests overall benefit but cannot say which strains work. The American Gastroenterological Association (2020) made no recommendation and advised using them only in trials. The World Gastroenterology Organization is more positive.
- **Safety:** Usually low risk in healthy people. Avoid in serious illness or weakened immunity; severe infections have occurred in premature infants.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Probiotics](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/probiotics-what-you-need-to-know), [AGA guideline news release (EurekAlert)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/485122), [ISAPP commentary on AGA guideline](https://isappscience.org/?p=3721)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker + journal: "4-week trial" template with a before/after symptom score; stop if no change.

### Probiotics with antibiotics
- **Use:** Prevent antibiotic-associated diarrhoea and C. difficile.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Take a probiotic during and shortly after an antibiotic course.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Reviews show about half the risk of antibiotic diarrhoea in non-hospitalised people (moderate-quality studies). There is moderate-certainty evidence of fewer C. difficile infections. The best strain and dose are unclear.
- **Safety:** As above; avoid if immunocompromised.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Probiotics](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/probiotics-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Household medicine log: when an antibiotic is logged, suggest "consider asking your pharmacist about a probiotic".

### "Gut reset" protocols sold with consumer microbiome tests
- **Use:** Fix a "dysbiosis" score from a stool test.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A test plus personalised supplements, prebiotics or antimicrobial herbs sold by the same company.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (the test basis is unreliable). Identical samples get different scores from different labs, and even from the same lab on repeat runs. The companies selling the fix have a financial conflict of interest.
- **Safety:** Cost. Antimicrobial herb "cleanses" can cause side effects and interactions.
- **Sources:** [NIST: Home gut microbiome tests give varying results](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/home-gut-microbiome-tests-give-varying-results), [Science News: Can you trust gut microbiome tests?](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/results-gut-microbiome-tests-trust)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: offer the food-based "feed your gut" alternative (30 plants a week, fibre, fermented foods) as a meal-planner challenge.

## Herbal and topical remedies

### Enteric-coated peppermint oil
- **Use:** IBS pain and global symptoms.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Enteric-coated capsules (typically 180-225 mg) up to three times daily before meals.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2022 meta-analysis found it better than placebo (NNT 4) but rated the evidence very low quality. Earlier meta-analyses were more positive.
- **Safety:** Heartburn is common. Avoid with reflux disease. More adverse events than placebo.
- **Sources:** [Ingrosso et al. 2022 meta-analysis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35942669/), [Alammar et al. 2019 meta-analysis (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6337770/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: "IBS flare toolkit" entry with a symptom tracker.

### Castor oil packs
- **Use:** Constipation, "liver detox", period pain, fertility (popular on social media).
- **What it is / how it's used:** A flannel soaked in castor oil placed on the abdomen under a heat source for 30-60 minutes.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. One small 2011 trial in elderly rest-home residents reported fewer constipation symptoms. There is no evidence for detox or fertility claims. Oral castor oil is a real laxative, but that does not carry over to packs. Warmth and abdominal massage may explain any effect.
- **Safety:** Low risk topically; possible skin irritation. Do not take castor oil by mouth in pregnancy (it can trigger contractions).
- **Sources:** [Arslan et al. 2011, castor oil packs and constipation in the elderly (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21168117/), [Lead Stories fact check](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2023/04/fact-check-castor-oil-compress-not-proven-to-treat-diarrhea-constipation.html)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: a "warm compress + abdominal massage" self-care routine for constipation, honestly labelled.

### Colon cleansing / colonic irrigation
- **Use:** "Detox", bloating, constipation.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Colon hydrotherapy sessions or high-dose laxative cleanse kits.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. Evidence is limited and side effects can be serious.
- **Safety:** Higher risk with bowel disease, previous colon surgery, kidney or heart disease. Risk of dehydration and electrolyte imbalance.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Detoxes and Cleanses](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library warning card.
