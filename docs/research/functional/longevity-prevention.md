# Longevity & prevention: functional and integrative approaches

Functional lab testing, "optimal range" biomarker culture and preventive supplement protocols: what is sold, and what the evidence shows.

## Functional lab testing

### Large "root cause" lab panels (blood, urine, stool)
- **Use:** Find hidden imbalances behind fatigue, gut or hormone symptoms.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Private clinics order dozens of analytes, often judged against narrow "optimal" ranges instead of standard reference ranges. In Sweden these panels can cost thousands to tens of thousands of SEK.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal (no evidence that broad panels improve outcomes). Statistically, about 5% of healthy people fall outside any reference range for each test, so with 40+ analytes most people get several "abnormal" results. VoF's 2025 review of Funmed names over-testing as the main departure from evidence-based care.
- **Safety:** Costly. False positives lead to restrictive diets, supplement stacks and anxiety. Real results can be over-interpreted or missed.
- **Sources:** [VoF: Utförlig information om Funmed (PDF)](https://vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Utforlig-information-om-Funmed.pdf), [VoF/Folkvett: Funktionell medicin (PDF)](https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/5-funktionll-medicin.pdf)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results: store any lab value with its own reference range and show "outside range ≠ disease". Add a prompt: "What would you do differently with this result?"

### IgG / IgG4 "food sensitivity" tests
- **Use:** Marketed to find foods causing bloating, fatigue, migraine, eczema, weight gain.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A blood test measuring IgG antibodies to 100+ foods, followed by eliminating the "positive" foods.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. EAACI (2008), the AAAAI (endorsed 2010), German societies and the Canadian CSACI (2012) all advise against it. IgG4 reflects normal exposure to a food (you make antibodies to what you eat), not intolerance. Positive results are expected in healthy adults and children.
- **Safety:** Unnecessary elimination diets can cause nutritional gaps (especially in children), disordered eating, and missed real diagnoses (coeliac disease, IgE allergy).
- **Sources:** [CSACI position statement on food-specific IgG testing](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3443017/), [EAACI Task Force: IgG4 testing not recommended](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18489614/), [AAAAI endorsement of EAACI paper (PDF)](https://www.aaaai.org/aaaai/media/medialibrary/pdf%20documents/practice%20and%20parameters/eacci-igg4-2010.pdf)
- **HealthHub fit:** Lab results / journal: warn when "IgG food test" is entered; suggest a food-and-symptom diary plus a clinician-guided elimination-reintroduction instead.

### Direct-to-consumer gut microbiome stool tests
- **Use:** "Know your gut", get personalised diet and probiotic advice.
- **What it is / how it's used:** At-home stool kits that sequence bacteria and score gut health, often bundled with supplements for sale.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (for decision-making). A 2026 NIST and University of Maryland study sent identical samples to 7 companies: results differed as much between companies as between different people, and one company labelled the same sample "healthy" twice and "unhealthy" once.
- **Safety:** Money spent on unreliable reports and the products that come with them. The researchers advise against using the results to inform lifestyle or medical decisions.
- **Sources:** [NIST: Home gut microbiome tests give varying results](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/home-gut-microbiome-tests-give-varying-results), [Scientific American: same sample, dramatically different results](https://scientificamerican.com/article/at-home-microbiome-tests-reveal-dramatically-different-results), [GenomeWeb: Buyer beware](https://www.genomeweb.com/genetic-research/buyer-beware-consumer-marketed-gut-microbiome-test-results-vary-wildly-new-study)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library / lab results: "consumer test reliability" badge (unreliable).

### Salivary cortisol "adrenal stress" profiles
- **Use:** Diagnose "adrenal fatigue" or "HPA axis dysfunction".
- **What it is / how it's used:** 4-point saliva or dried-urine cortisol curves sold by functional labs, followed by adrenal "support" supplements or glandulars.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. A 2016 systematic review of 58 studies concluded that "adrenal fatigue does not exist": cortisol test results were inconsistent and the methods are not endorsed by endocrinologists. No endocrine society recognises the diagnosis.
- **Safety:** Adrenal glandular products can contain real hormones. Real adrenal insufficiency, thyroid disease, depression or sleep apnoea can be missed.
- **Sources:** [Cadegiani & Kater, Adrenal fatigue does not exist: a systematic review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4997656/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: a "fatigue checklist" that points to real causes to discuss with a doctor (iron, thyroid, sleep, mood), plus a sleep/energy tracker.

## Preventive supplements

### Vitamin D "for everyone" to prevent cancer and heart disease
- **Use:** General prevention; one of the most common functional medicine recommendations.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Daily vitamin D3 (often 2,000-5,000 IU), often titrated to an "optimal" blood level.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (for preventing heart disease and cancer incidence in the general population). The VITAL trial (25,871 adults, 2,000 IU/day for about 5 years) found no reduction in cancer incidence (HR 0.96) or major cardiovascular events (HR 0.97). Meta-analyses including VITAL suggest a possible reduction in cancer deaths. Correcting a real deficiency is a separate question.
- **Safety:** 2,000 IU/day caused no excess high calcium in VITAL. Very high doses can cause toxicity.
- **Sources:** [NCI Cancer Currents: VITAL results](https://www.cancer.gov/news-events/cancer-currents-blog/2018/vitamin-d-supplement-cancer-prevention), [VITAL principal results and updated meta-analyses (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31733345/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker: show "evidence for: deficiency/winter in the Nordics; not shown for: heart/cancer prevention".

### Omega-3 fish oil for prevention
- **Use:** Heart, brain, "inflammation".
- **What it is / how it's used:** 1 g/day EPA+DHA capsules, or eating oily fish 1-2 times a week.
- **Evidence:** Promising. VITAL found no reduction in its primary outcomes (cancer, major cardiovascular events) but 28% fewer heart attacks as a secondary outcome. Reviews show small reductions in coronary events, mostly in people with heart disease or high triglycerides. It did not help macular degeneration (AREDS2) or dry eye (DREAM).
- **Safety:** Usually mild (fishy taste, heartburn). Tell your clinician if you take blood thinners.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Omega-3 Supplements](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/omega3-supplements-what-you-need-to-know), [Healio: VITAL mixed findings](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20181207/vital-vitamin-d-omega3-fail-to-reduce-major-cv-events-cancer)
- **HealthHub fit:** Meal planner: "oily fish twice a week" goal with salmon/herring/mackerel recipes as the food-first option.

## Movement for healthy ageing

### Tai chi for balance and fall prevention
- **Use:** Prevent falls in older adults; balance in Parkinson's disease.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Slow, flowing Chinese mind-body exercise, typically 1-3 classes a week for 12+ weeks.
- **Evidence:** Promising (moderate certainty in older adults; varies by population). It may also help knee osteoarthritis, COPD and cognition in mild cognitive impairment.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Adverse events are similar to control groups (minor aches). Pregnant people should check first.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Tai Chi: What You Need To Know](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/tai-chi-what-you-need-to-know)
