# Sleep: functional and integrative approaches

Supplements, herbs and mind-body practices promoted for sleep, with what trials and sleep guidelines actually say.

## Supplements

### Magnesium
- **Use:** Trouble falling asleep, "calming" mineral.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Magnesium citrate, glycinate or oxide in the evening. Trials used 320-729 mg elemental magnesium per day.
- **Evidence:** Promising (weak). A 2021 meta-analysis of 3 RCTs in 151 older adults found people fell asleep about 17 minutes faster. Total sleep time did not change significantly. Evidence quality was low to very low.
- **Safety:** Diarrhoea at higher doses. Avoid supplements with kidney disease. Can reduce absorption of some antibiotics and osteoporosis drugs (space doses apart).
- **Sources:** [Mah & Pitre 2021, oral magnesium for insomnia in older adults (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8053283/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker + sleep log: a 4-week n-of-1 experiment comparing time to fall asleep on and off magnesium.

### Melatonin
- **Use:** Jet lag, shift work, delayed sleep phase ("night owl"), insomnia.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 0.5-5 mg before the target bedtime. In Sweden it is prescription-only medicine at most doses.
- **Evidence:** Promising for jet lag (especially flying east) and delayed sleep phase. Two guidelines (2017, 2019) recommend against it for chronic insomnia.
- **Safety:** Mild drowsiness and headache. US gummies are often mislabelled (74-347% of the stated dose). CDC estimates about 11,000 emergency visits (2019-2022) from children 5 and under accidentally eating melatonin. Long-term effects in children are unknown.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Melatonin](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know), [NCCIH: Sleep Disorders In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sleep-disorders-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Travel routine: a jet-lag planner (light exposure + melatonin timing) and a "keep out of reach" household reminder.

### Ashwagandha for sleep
- **Use:** Stress-related poor sleep.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Root extract, usually 300-600 mg/day for 8+ weeks.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Benefits for sleep are small. NIH summaries note it seemed most helpful at ≥600 mg/day for ≥8 weeks.
- **Safety:** May be safe for up to 3 months. Rare but documented liver injury (avoid with liver disease). May affect thyroid function and thyroid medication. Avoid in pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Ashwagandha](https://nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha), [LiverTox: Ashwagandha](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548536/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Supplement tracker with a 12-week maximum reminder and a liver-symptom warning.

## Herbs

### Valerian
- **Use:** Insomnia.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Root extract or tea at bedtime. Sold in Sweden as a traditional herbal medicine.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Trials are inconsistent and meta-analyses conflict (with signs of publication bias). The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (2017) recommends against it for chronic insomnia.
- **Safety:** Do not combine with alcohol or sedatives. Long-term safety is uncertain.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Valerian](https://nccih.nih.gov/health/valerian), [Bent et al. 2006 meta-analysis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17145239/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library entry with an "evidence mixed" label.

### Chamomile tea
- **Use:** Winding down before bed.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 1-2 cups of tea in the evening.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. No conclusive trial evidence for insomnia.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Possible allergy in people sensitive to ragweed.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Sleep Disorders In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sleep-disorders-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine: a caffeine-free "evening tea" step in a wind-down routine (the ritual itself may help).

### Lavender aromatherapy
- **Use:** Relaxation and sleep.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Essential oil diffuser or pillow spray.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Aromatherapy reviews suggest possible benefit, but there is no direct evidence for insomnia.
- **Safety:** Low risk for adults. Some children using lavender products developed breast-tissue swelling (cause unclear), so be careful with children.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Sleep Disorders In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sleep-disorders-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine step (optional), flagged "adults only".

### Kava
- **Use:** Anxiety-related insomnia.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Root drink or extract.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. Little evidence for insomnia, and linked to serious and fatal liver injury.
- **Safety:** Avoid.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Sleep Disorders In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sleep-disorders-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library warning card.

## Mind-body and physical practices

### Mindfulness meditation for sleep
- **Use:** Insomnia, racing thoughts at night.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 10-30 minutes of daily mindfulness practice, or an 8-week MBSR-style course.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2019 analysis of 18 studies found better sleep quality than education-based treatments, and similar to CBT or exercise.
- **Safety:** Low risk. About 8% report a negative effect (e.g. anxiety), similar to psychological therapies.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Meditation and Mindfulness: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine: a guided "body scan" step in the evening routine, linked to the sleep log.

### Yoga and tai chi for sleep
- **Use:** Poor sleep quality, especially in older adults, people with cancer and women in menopause.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Gentle yoga or tai chi 2-3 times a week, or a short evening flow.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Multiple studies show better sleep quality, but not necessarily improvement in diagnosed insomnia.
- **Safety:** Low risk. Avoid hot yoga and extreme poses as a beginner.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Yoga: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety), [NCCIH: Sleep Disorders In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sleep-disorders-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routine: a 10-minute "bedtime yoga" timer routine.

### Acupuncture (including ear acupuncture) for insomnia
- **Use:** Insomnia.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Weekly sessions with a practitioner; ear seeds or ear acupressure at home.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Reviews are positive but based on small, low-quality studies. Guidelines found the evidence insufficient, with only a weak recommendation for ear acupuncture.
- **Safety:** Rare but serious harms with poor technique or non-sterile needles.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Sleep Disorders In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sleep-disorders-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library entry only.

### Cold-water immersion for sleep
- **Use:** Better sleep (popular "biohack").
- **What it is / how it's used:** Cold showers or plunges, usually earlier in the day.
- **Evidence:** Promising (narrow). A 2025 meta-analysis found improved sleep quality, but those data came only from men.
- **Safety:** Cold shock risk; avoid with heart conditions.
- **Sources:** [Cain et al., PLOS One 2025 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879231/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Self-experiment: cold-shower days vs. sleep-score correlation in the dashboard.

### Grounding sheets for sleep
- **Use:** Claimed deeper sleep and lower night-time cortisol.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Conductive bedsheets connected to an outlet's earth pin.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. Tiny pilot studies only.
- **Safety:** Low risk if wired correctly; wasted money.
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic: Earthing](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/earthing), [Science-Based Medicine: Earthing update](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/earthing-update/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: note it as "unproven"; no product links.
