# Women's health: functional and integrative approaches

"Hormone balancing" protocols, herbs and mind-body practices for the menstrual cycle, PMS, period pain, PCOS, fertility and menopause.

## Cycle and "hormone balancing"

### Seed cycling
- **Use:** "Balance hormones", regulate cycles, ease PMS and PCOS (very popular on social media and in functional nutrition).
- **What it is / how it's used:** 1 tbsp each of ground flax and pumpkin seeds daily in the follicular phase (days 1-14), then sesame and sunflower seeds in the luteal phase.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal. No study shows that rotating seeds by cycle phase changes hormone levels. The seeds themselves add fibre, lignans, zinc and healthy fats.
- **Safety:** Low risk (avoid with seed allergies). Some versions add evening primrose oil, which may interact with blood thinners.
- **Sources:** [GoodRx: Seed cycling](https://www.goodrx.com/health-topic/womens-health/seed-cycling-hormone), [News-Medical: Is seed cycling legit?](https://www.news-medical.net/health/Is-Seed-Cycling-Legit-for-Hormone-Balance.aspx), [The Food Medic: Can seed cycling balance hormones?](https://www.thefoodmedic.co.uk/can-seed-cycling-actually-balance-our-hormones)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle tracker: optional phase-based seed reminder labelled "unproven but nutritious"; pair with symptom logging so users can judge for themselves.

### Vitex agnus-castus (chasteberry, munkpeppar)
- **Use:** PMS (breast tenderness, irritability, bloating), PMDD.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Standardised fruit extract (often 20-40 mg/day) for at least 3 cycles. Available in Sweden as an approved herbal medicine.
- **Evidence:** Promising. A 2017 meta-analysis of 14 trials found a large pooled effect, but high risk of bias and publication bias mean it is probably overestimated. All 8 higher-quality trials in another review were positive.
- **Safety:** Mild, infrequent side effects. Avoid in pregnancy and with hormonal contraception or fertility treatment unless advised. May interact with dopamine-related drugs.
- **Sources:** [Verkaik et al. 2017 meta-analysis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28237870/), [Planta Medica 2013 systematic review (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23136064/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle tracker: a 3-cycle PMS symptom score so a user can see whether a remedy helped.

### Ginger for period pain
- **Use:** Primary dysmenorrhoea.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 750-2,000 mg ginger powder daily for the first 3-4 days of the period.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Several meta-analyses show less pain than placebo, roughly comparable to NSAIDs in a few small trials. Trial quality is weak and safety reporting is sparse.
- **Safety:** Heartburn. Use caution with blood thinners at high doses.
- **Sources:** [Ginger for primary dysmenorrhea: 2024 meta-analysis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38770631/), [Daily et al. 2015, Pain Medicine](https://academic.oup.com/painmedicine/article/16/12/2243/2460294)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes + cycle: ginger tea and recipes suggested on predicted period days 1-3.

### Myo-inositol for PCOS
- **Use:** Insulin resistance, irregular cycles, hirsutism in PCOS.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Myo-inositol powder (often 2-4 g/day), sometimes with D-chiro-inositol.
- **Evidence:** Promising. The meta-analysis behind the 2023 international PCOS guideline (30 trials) found limited, inconclusive evidence: possible improvements in waist-hip ratio and hirsutism, and likely no difference in reproductive outcomes. It has fewer GI side effects than metformin. Another 2023 meta-analysis is more positive.
- **Safety:** Generally well tolerated.
- **Sources:** [Fitz et al., inositol for PCOS to inform 2023 guideline (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11099481/), [Healio: More studies needed on inositol](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20240410/more-studies-needed-to-assess-effects-of-inositol-on-pcos-outcomes)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle + supplement tracker: cycle-length trend view for users with PCOS.

### Castor oil packs for fertility or cramps
- **Use:** Claimed to "detox" the uterus, support fertility, ease cramps.
- **What it is / how it's used:** A warm castor-oil cloth on the lower abdomen.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal (no trials for fertility).
- **Safety:** Low risk topically. Do not use while trying to conceive after ovulation or in pregnancy without advice. Never take castor oil by mouth in pregnancy.
- **Sources:** [Lead Stories fact check](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2023/04/fact-check-castor-oil-compress-not-proven-to-treat-diarrhea-constipation.html)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library: list under "heat for cramps"; a heat pad is the evidence-friendly alternative.

## Interactions with contraception

### St John's wort and the contraceptive pill
- **Use:** Safety warning (St John's wort is used for low mood).
- **What it is / how it's used:** n/a
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (as a combination). RCTs show lower progestin levels, more breakthrough bleeding and signs of ovulation. Case reports of unplanned pregnancy. 1177 warns that the effect lasts about 2 weeks after stopping.
- **Safety:** Use other contraception or avoid the combination.
- **Sources:** [RCT: St John's wort and low-dose oral contraceptives (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14616430/), [1177: Växtbaserade läkemedel och naturläkemedel](https://www.1177.se/undersokning-behandling/fler-behandlingar/alternativa-behandlingar/vaxtbaserade-lakemedel-och-naturlakemedel/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Cycle tracker: if contraception is set to "pill" and St John's wort is logged, show a warning.

## Fertility

### Acupuncture alongside IVF
- **Use:** Improve IVF pregnancy rates.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Sessions around embryo transfer.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (no benefit). A 2021 review (6 studies, 2,507 people) found no difference vs sham in pregnancy or live birth rates.
- **Safety:** Low physical risk; cost and false hope.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Acupuncture: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/acupuncture-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library evidence note.

## Pregnancy

### Herbal supplements and herbal teas in pregnancy and breastfeeding
- **Use:** Safety guidance.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Livsmedelsverket has assessed the risks of herbal supplements and some herbal teas during pregnancy and breastfeeding. 1177 generally advises against herbal medicines then unless a doctor recommends them.
- **Evidence:** Proven (national authority guidance).
- **Safety:** Avoid concentrated herbal products unless a midwife or doctor approves.
- **Sources:** [Livsmedelsverket L 2021 nr 25 (PDF)](https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/globalassets/publikationsdatabas/rapporter/2021/l-2021-nr-25-risker-med-vaxtbaserade-kosttillskott-och-ortteer-under-graviditet-och-amning.pdf), [1177: Växtbaserade läkemedel och naturläkemedel](https://www.1177.se/undersokning-behandling/fler-behandlingar/alternativa-behandlingar/vaxtbaserade-lakemedel-och-naturlakemedel/)
- **HealthHub fit:** Profile: a "pregnant/breastfeeding" flag that adds warnings to every herbal remedy and supplement entry.

## Menopause

### Black cohosh
- **Use:** Hot flushes, night sweats.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Root extract (e.g. 20-40 mg twice daily).
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal (trials inconclusive). A 2012 Cochrane review (16 RCTs, 2,027 women) found insufficient evidence of benefit.
- **Safety:** Rare but serious liver damage (at least 83 reported cases). Stop if you get dark urine, abdominal pain or jaundice.
- **Sources:** [Cochrane: Black cohosh for menopausal symptoms](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007244/MENSTR_black-cohosh-cimicifuga-spp.-for-menopausal-symptoms), [NIH ODS: Black Cohosh fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/BlackCohosh-HealthProfessional), [NCCIH: Menopausal Symptoms In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/menopausal-symptoms-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library entry with a liver warning.

### Soy and red clover isoflavones
- **Use:** Hot flushes.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Soy foods or isoflavone supplements.
- **Evidence:** Promising (inconsistent results across trials).
- **Safety:** Likely safe short-term. Long-term safety is unknown; may be unsuitable for people who should avoid oestrogen.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Menopausal Symptoms In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/menopausal-symptoms-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Recipes: soy-based meals (tofu, edamame) as a food-first option.

### Clinical hypnosis for hot flushes
- **Use:** Frequent hot flushes, poor sleep.
- **What it is / how it's used:** 5 weekly sessions with a trained provider plus daily audio self-hypnosis.
- **Evidence:** Promising. An NCCIH-funded trial found fewer hot flushes, better sleep and less interference with daily life.
- **Safety:** Generally safe with trained providers.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Menopausal Symptoms In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/menopausal-symptoms-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines: evening self-hypnosis/relaxation audio; hot-flush counter in the cycle/menopause tracker.

### Mindfulness and yoga in menopause
- **Use:** How much hot flushes bother you, anxiety, sleep.
- **What it is / how it's used:** MBSR course; yoga 2-3 times a week.
- **Evidence:** Promising. Mindfulness made hot flushes less bothersome (not less intense). A 2018 review of 13 yoga studies found reduced physical and psychological symptoms.
- **Safety:** Low risk.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Menopausal Symptoms In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/menopausal-symptoms-in-depth), [NCCIH: Yoga: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **HealthHub fit:** Routines tagged "menopause".

### Acupuncture for menopause
- **Use:** Hot flushes.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Weekly sessions.
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful (not better than sham). Beat no treatment in some studies, but no better than simulated acupuncture and less effective than hormone therapy.
- **Safety:** Generally safe with sterile needles.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Menopausal Symptoms In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/menopausal-symptoms-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library evidence note.

### DHEA
- **Use:** Libido, energy, "hormone balance" in menopause.
- **What it is / how it's used:** Oral DHEA (sold online abroad; in Sweden it is treated as a medicine, not a food supplement, so check before importing).
- **Evidence:** Disproven / likely harmful. Benefit is uncertain, and a task force recommends against routine use.
- **Safety:** Long-term safety unknown; possible liver damage.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Menopausal Symptoms In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/menopausal-symptoms-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Remedy library warning card.

### Dong quai
- **Use:** "Female ginseng" for menopause and cramps (TCM).
- **What it is / how it's used:** Root tea or capsules.
- **Evidence:** Traditional / anecdotal (very little research).
- **Safety:** May interact with warfarin.
- **Sources:** [NCCIH: Menopausal Symptoms In Depth](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/menopausal-symptoms-in-depth)
- **HealthHub fit:** Interaction flag in the supplement tracker.
